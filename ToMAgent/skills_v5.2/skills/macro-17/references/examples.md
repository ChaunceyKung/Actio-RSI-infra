# macro-17 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
given that harm has occurred, which repair move (acknowledgement, apology, amends, prevention) is proportional to the injury and best restores the relationship

## Route Signal
An offense or harm has already happened, someone is hurt, and the scene asks how to apologize, compensate, or make things right.

## Hard Boundary
- If no one has actually been harmed yet, there is nothing to repair and this is not the right scene.
- If the task is only to clarify what someone meant before any damage, restating intent (not apologizing) is the move.
- If the task is only to judge whether an offense occurred, answer that judgment; do not perform a repair.
- If the task is reaching a mutually acceptable trade-off, that is bargaining, not making amends.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not lead with an explanation or excuse when the harm has not yet been acknowledged from the victim's side.
- Do not offer a bare 'sorry' with no amends when the injury is real enough to require restitution or changed behavior.
- Do not treat the response as routine politeness; the relationship is now in a damaged state that must be actively restored.
- Do not apologize at all when nothing was actually harmed; that misreads a clarification or a non-event as an offense.

## Common Failure Modes
- Apologizing or over-repairing when no real harm occurred (e.g., treating a mere misunderstanding or a correct statement as an offense).
- Giving a hollow apology with no concrete amends when the harm clearly calls for restitution or changed behavior.
- Misjudging severity: under-repairing a serious wound, or over-repairing a trivial slight so the apology looks insincere.
- Defending, justifying ('I was just being honest'), or shifting blame instead of acknowledging the harm and repairing it.
- Confusing the repair task with merely detecting that an offense occurred, or with negotiating terms.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A person bluntly insults a friend's gift, the friend is visibly hurt, and the person must choose how to respond. | Why: Real harm has occurred and the task is to repair it; the right move is to acknowledge the hurt, apologize, and make it right.
- Case: A person reads a story and is asked only whether anyone said something inappropriate, or which sentence was inappropriate. | Why: This is detecting that an offense occurred, a judgment about what happened, not the live repair of a damaged relationship, so it belongs to the offense-detection scene, not here.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If no offense or hurt has actually happened, do not route here even if the words 'sorry' or 'mistake' appear.
- If the question is only whether or which line was inappropriate, that is offense detection, not repair.
- If the apology language is present but the real question is how to split costs, concessions, or a plan, that is negotiation.
- If a misunderstanding is still correctable before anyone is harmed, the move is clarifying intent, not apologizing.

## Generalization Note
This macro covers repair behavior after damage: acknowledgement, apology, restitution or compensation, reassurance, and prevention. It applies to any scene whose primary goal is to restore a relationship AFTER an offense or harm has occurred, with the repair calibrated to the severity of that harm.
