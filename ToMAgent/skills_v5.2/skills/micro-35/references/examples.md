# micro-35 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
what the utterance is doing socially

## Route Signal
Use this unit when an utterance must be classified by the social action it performs in context, especially indirect requests, refusals, reminders, warnings, hints, invitations, or mockery.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit answers what the utterance DOES, not what it SAYS (explicit content is the Literal Meaning capability, micro-34).
- It names an act, not an extra implied proposition (a quantity or hidden fact is the Conversational Implicature capability, micro-36).
- It does not judge whether the content matches the facts (that is the Truth, Lie, and Half-Truth capability, micro-37).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not answer with the literal paraphrase when the utterance clearly serves to make the listener act.
- Do not relabel the act as an implied fact: 'is asking for water' is an act; 'there is no water here' would be an implication.
- Do not over-read the speaker's inner motive when the task only asks which act is performed.
- Do not collapse a distinct act (warning, reminder) into a bare statement of fact.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering the literal semantic content instead of the speech act (staying at SAY when the task wants DO).
- Labeling the case as implied information when the real target is the action type.
- Treating a warning or reminder as merely a neutral statement of fact.
- Missing that a question form ('Can't you find the cleaner?') is functioning as a request or order.
- Confusing mockery or sarcasm with a sincere compliment when identifying the act.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Marissa, finding the bathroom dirty after Anna's bath, calls out 'Anna, can't you find the cleaner?', and the question asks what Marissa really wants. | Why: The literal form is a yes/no question, but in context it performs the act of getting Anna to clean the bathroom; the diagnostic target is the act, so it is in scope for this capability.
- Case: A caller reports 'I checked 3 of the 5 letters; 2 had checks,' and the question asks how many of all five letters contain checks. The target is an inferred quantity, not an act performed on a listener. | Why: Here the target is an extra inferred quantity, not an act performed on a listener, so it routes to the implied-information capability (Conversational Implicature, micro-36), not to this unit.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same words were spoken in a different context, would the social act change? If yes, the act is what you must name.
- Can you name the act (request, warning, reminder, refusal, hint, mockery) without judging truth, motive, or any implied extra fact?
- Does the literal reading alone miss the point of the utterance? If so, this unit applies; if the literal reading IS the point, route to the explicit-meaning capability (Literal Meaning, micro-34).

## Generalization Note
This is the DO member of the pragmatics triad: SAY (Literal Meaning, micro-34), DO (this unit), IMPLY (Conversational Implicature, micro-36). It generalizes to any case where an utterance's form and function diverge and the task is to classify the communicative act, not the proposition or its truth.
