# micro-39 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the speaker's social motive for choosing that utterance form

## Route Signal
Use this capability when the prompt asks why a speaker chose a particular utterance, and the best answer is a social motive: sparing feelings, saving face, politeness, harmony, humor, testing, avoidance, or ingratiation.

## Hard Boundary
- Not a verdict on whether the statement is true, false, or a half-truth (that is the truth-status capability, micro-37).
- Not whether a true statement creates a false impression (that is the misleading-expression capability, micro-38).
- Not how to change the listener's mind (that is the persuasion-strategy capability, micro-41).
- Not fitting wording to the listener's level (that is the audience-calibration capability, micro-40).
- Not decoding the implied meaning or hidden request (that is the implied-meaning capability, micro-36, or the indirect-speech-act capability, micro-35).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not answer 'why' with a truth verdict ('because it is true' / 'because it is a lie'); name the social motive.
- Do not pick the option that says the praise is completely sincere when the story shows a private contrary feeling.
- Do not upgrade a polite or kind motive into persuasion unless the goal is clearly to change the listener.
- Do not infer audience adaptation just because the wording is gentle or simple.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with truth status ('it is a lie' / 'it is true') instead of the motive.
- Choosing the option that claims the speaker is sincere, ignoring the stated inner contrast.
- Treating a polite white lie as persuasion or as audience calibration.
- Treating a playful exaggeration meant to make the listener laugh as if it were a sincere literal claim.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Jianjun thinks his aunt's new hat is ugly but tells her 'it looks good'; asked why he says it, the answer is that he tells a white lie to spare her feelings. | Why: The question is 'Why does he say this?' and the decisive variable is the kind social motive (spare feelings), not the truth of the hat.
- Case: A child wants to go to the amusement park; her dad objects to the noise; the question is which appeal the child uses to bring him around. | Why: Near miss: the wording is chosen to change the listener's decision, so the central variable is the influence tactic. Route to the persuasion-strategy capability (micro-41).

## Boundary Stress Test
- Same sentence, but the question is whether it is true or a lie: route to the truth-status capability (micro-37).
- Same sentence, but the question is what the speaker really means or hints: route to the implied-meaning capability (micro-36) or indirect-speech-act capability (micro-35).
- Same sentence, but chosen to make a child understand it: route to the audience-calibration capability (micro-40).
- Same sentence, but chosen to convince someone to act: route to the persuasion-strategy capability (micro-41).

## Generalization Note
This capability generalizes to any utterance where the key variable is the speaker's social reason for choosing that form — including white lies told to spare feelings or keep harmony, polite untruths, and playful exaggerations — not the factual content, the implied meaning, or the listener's comprehension.
