# micro-19 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the character's own preference

## Route Signal
Route here when the diagnostic question is THIS character's own liking, wanting, or self-directed choice — and the answer depends on their desire alone, not on anyone else's mind.

## Hard Boundary
- Another person's preference, or this character accommodating it, belongs to modeling another person's preference (micro-20) — not here.
- Uncertainty about a preference belongs to certainty of a belief (micro-17) or source credibility (micro-18).
- Ranking goals, duties, or constraints that override liking belongs to goal hierarchy (micro-21), commitment priority (micro-24), or goal-conflict arbitration (micro-25).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not treat a polite or face-saving statement (e.g. 'No, I do not want to') as the true preference when the scene shows the character actually wants the opposite.
- Do not answer from what is useful, expected, or good for the group — answer from what the character themselves wants.
- Do not let a brief interruption (an invitation, a distraction) erase the original desire the character set aside.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing the character's own preference with their guess about, or accommodation of, someone else's preference.
- Treating a duty or constraint the character obeys as if it were their liking.
- Reading a polite denial as the genuine wish, ignoring the desire the story makes explicit.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Susan wants to eat the cookies she baked, but a friend invites her to swim; she puts the cookies back and goes swimming. After she comes home, what does she do? — she eats her cookies. | Why: The query is Susan's OWN desire, which persists across the interruption. Answer depends only on her preference, so this is Self Preference.
- Case: Adventurous Li Hua invites quiet, library-loving Wang Fang for the weekend. Where does Li Hua plan to spend it with her? — the Library. | Why: The answer is driven by Li Hua's model of Wang Fang's quiet taste, NOT his own adventurous liking. This routes to modeling another person's preference (micro-20).

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the scene asks 'What does she think HE likes?' or 'Where should she take HIM to please him?', do not use this unit — it is modeling another person's preference (micro-20).
- If the scene asks 'What does she want, judging from her own repeated choice?', use this unit.
- If the character likes X but does Y because of a duty or to avoid being mocked, the question 'why did she do Y' is about which goal won — exit to commitment priority or goal-conflict arbitration (micro-24/micro-25), not here.

## Generalization Note
This unit generalizes to any self-directed liking or wanting — food, objects, activities, partners, plans, options — whether stable or momentary, as long as the answer rests on the focal character's OWN desire.
