# Other Preference

## Use When
- Use when the question asks what one character thinks ANOTHER person likes, wants, or would choose.
- Use when a character chooses, recommends, buys, or plans FOR someone else, and the right answer suits that other person's taste.
- Use when two people have different tastes and the answer must accommodate the OTHER person's preference rather than the chooser's own.
- Use when the answer changes depending on whose mind you read — and the queried mind is someone other than the actor's own.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the question is about the focal character's OWN liking or wanting — that is the character's own preference (micro-19).
- Do not use when the task is to infer the PURPOSE behind an action, gaze, gesture, or request — that is intention recognition (micro-22).
- Do not use when the required answer is what someone will DO next as a behavior, not what they LIKE — that is action prediction (micro-23).
- Do not use when the deciding cue is belief, emotion, trust, or deception rather than preference.

## Decision Variable
the character's representation of another person's preference

## Trigger Checklist
- Is the queried liking someone OTHER than the actor's — a friend, partner, customer, child, guest?
- Does the actor have to set aside their own taste and pick what suits the OTHER person?
- Can the answer be reached by matching the other person's known taste, prior choices, or stable likes?
- Is the queried thing a LIKING (what the other wants), not the purpose of an act or a prediction of the next move?

## Workflow
- Identify whose preference is being asked about and confirm it is a person OTHER than the actor.
- Read that other person's taste from the scene: stated likes, stable disposition, or prior choices.
- If the actor's own taste differs, explicitly set it aside — the answer must match the OTHER person, not the actor.
- Choose the option that best fits the other person's preference; reject options that merely suit the actor or that describe an action's purpose.
- Exit if the question is really about the actor's own taste, the purpose behind an act, or a predicted next behavior (see Boundary Exit Rule).

## Special Case
**Set aside the actor's own taste**: When a character chooses for someone else AND has a different personal taste, the answer follows the OTHER person's preference. The actor's own liking is a distractor, not the answer.

Scene: Adventurous, nature-loving Li Hua often hikes on weekends. His friend Wang Min is an interior designer who prefers indoor activities, especially art exhibitions and design expos. Wang Min wants to invite Li Hua to spend the weekend together and make it enjoyable for him.
Question: To make this weekend enjoyable, where should Wang Min suggest going?
Answer logic: Wang Min must accommodate Li Hua's nature/photography taste, not her own indoor taste, so she suggests a Natural Landscape Photography Exhibition — a bridge that fits Li Hua's preference. The decisive variable is Wang Min's representation of Li Hua's liking.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the question asks what the focal character THEMSELVES wants or likes, exit to the character's own preference capability — their own taste (micro-19).
- If the main task is to infer the PURPOSE behind an action, look, gesture, or request, exit to intention recognition — why they did it (micro-22).
- If the required answer is the other person's next BEHAVIOR rather than their taste, exit to action prediction — what they will do next (micro-23).
- If the deciding cue is trust, belief, emotion, or deception rather than what someone likes, exit to the relevant belief, emotion, or source-credibility capability.
