# Group Identity

## Use When
- Use when belonging to an in-group or out-group changes how an act, remark, or person is interpreted or expected to behave.
- Use when a stereotype, affiliation, or social category shifts the evaluation, so the same behavior would be read differently for someone in the other group.
- Use when the observer's inference is explained by which group the person belongs to rather than who that one person is.
- Use when the task is to pin down a group-membership effect and rule out interpersonal rank or one person's reliability history.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the driver is rank or authority between two individuals (who outranks whom, who is the boss or expert) rather than membership in a category; that is interpersonal hierarchy, not group identity (the Power, Status, and Role unit).
- Do not use when the judgment comes from one specific person's track record or broken promises rather than the group they belong to; that is reliability from personal history (the Trust and Reputation unit).
- Do not use when the issue is whether information may be shared or kept private; that is disclosure boundaries (the Privacy, Secrets, and Boundaries unit).
- Do not use when the issue is repair after offense, apology, or reconciliation; that is mending a relationship after harm (the Relationship Repair and Apology unit).
- Do not use when the question is plain belief, emotion, intention, or word meaning with no group-membership cue shaping the inference.

## Decision Variable
whether group membership changes interpretation or evaluation

## Trigger Checklist
- Is there a category label that splits people into us versus them (same school, team, club, nationality, profession, fan base, generation)?
- Would the SAME act or remark be read differently if the person belonged to the other category?
- Is the explanation a stereotype, in-group favoritism, or out-group suspicion attached to the whole category, not to that one individual?
- Is the driver membership in a group rather than one person's rank over another or one person's past record?

## Workflow
- Find the category cue: a label that sorts people into groups (in-group versus out-group, shared category, stereotype, belonging). If no such cue exists, this unit does not apply.
- Run the swap test: imagine the same person doing the same thing but belonging to the OTHER group. If the judgment flips, group membership is the decisive variable and this unit applies.
- Separate membership from rank: if the judgment turns on who outranks whom or who holds the authority or expertise between two people, it is interpersonal hierarchy, not group identity, so exit to the power and role unit.
- Separate membership from personal history: if the judgment comes from THIS person's own past actions or broken promises rather than their group, it is reliability from history, so exit to the trust and reputation unit.
- If group membership survives both checks, name the effect: state which group is treated as in-group or out-group and how that belonging or stereotype shifts the interpretation, expectation, or evaluation.

## Special Case
**Membership cue with no named stereotype**: The scene need not name an explicit stereotype. If a person is judged because of which group they belong to, and the same act would be read differently for an in-group member, this unit still applies.

Scene: Two students hand in late assignments with the same excuse. The teacher accepts it from a student in her own homeroom but doubts the student transferred in from the rival school across town.
Question: Why does the teacher treat the two excuses differently?
Answer logic: Run the swap test: the only thing that changed is which group the student belongs to. The teacher extends in-group benefit of the doubt and applies out-group suspicion. No stereotype is named and no rank or personal record is involved, so the decisive variable is group membership and this unit applies.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the question turns on rank, authority, or formal role between two people (who is the boss, who must defer) rather than which group someone belongs to, exit to the unit on power, status, and role structure between individuals.
- If the question turns on whether one specific person is reliable based on their own past actions rather than their group, exit to the unit on trust and reputation from personal history.
- If the question turns on whether information may be disclosed or concealed, exit to the unit on privacy, secrets, and boundaries.
- If the question turns on apology, harm repair, or restoring a relationship after offense, exit to the unit on relationship repair and apology.

## Quick Route Signal
Route here when which group a person belongs to (in-group versus out-group, shared category, stereotype, belonging) is what changes how their act, remark, or character is read. Not here if the decisive cue is one person's rank over another or one person's own track record.
