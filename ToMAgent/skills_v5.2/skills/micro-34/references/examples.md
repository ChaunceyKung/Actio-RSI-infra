# micro-34 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the sentence's explicit semantic content

## Route Signal
Use this unit when the diagnostic target is what the utterance EXPLICITLY says, the plain semantic content, independent of any hidden intention, social act, or implied extra meaning.

## Hard Boundary
- Literal Meaning stops at what the words conventionally encode (SAY), not what they DO or IMPLY.
- It does not state what the speaker wants the listener to do; that is the social act of the utterance (Indirect Speech Act, micro-35).
- It does not add unstated conclusions the listener is meant to infer; that is implied information (Conversational Implicature, micro-36).
- It does not judge whether the utterance is true, deceptive, persuasive, or comforting; those are separate capabilities.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not replace a polite question with the request it would perform; the literal content of a question is the question.
- Do not treat the most socially plausible reading as the literal meaning; literal = the words' conventional sense only.
- Do not add causal, emotional, or moral conclusions just because they are likely in the scene.
- Do not let an obvious hint leak into the literal paraphrase; keep the hint out and report only the words.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering the implied request or hint instead of the literal statement (jumping from SAY to DO).
- Reading an understated phrase ('a little salt') as the criticism it implies instead of its plain meaning.
- Treating sarcasm, politeness, or tact as part of the literal sentence content.
- Using the speaker's likely motive to rewrite what the sentence literally says.
- Overusing context to infer beyond simple reference resolution.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A sign reads 'The museum closes at 6 p.m.' and the question asks what the sign says. Answer: the museum closes at 6 p.m. | Why: The target is the explicit posted content, read straight off the words; this is in scope for literal meaning.
- Case: A guard points to that same sign and tells lingering visitors 'The museum closes at 6 p.m.', and the question asks what the guard is trying to get them to do. | Why: Same words, but the target is now the social act (an indirect request or warning to leave), so it routes to the social-action-of-an-utterance capability (Indirect Speech Act, micro-35), not to literal meaning.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If an utterance is literally false but socially useful, keep the literal content separate from its social function.
- If an utterance is literally a question but conventionally used as a request, report only the question form unless the task asks for the act.
- If context makes a hidden meaning obvious, deliberately exclude it from the literal paraphrase.
- If a word is ambiguous, use stated context only to pick its referent or sense, never to import a broader unstated message.

## Generalization Note
This is the semantic baseline of the pragmatics triad: SAY (literal) here, versus DO (social act, Indirect Speech Act, micro-35) and IMPLY (extra inferred content, Conversational Implicature, micro-36). Always state the explicit encoded meaning first, then route out if the task wants the act, the implication, or the truth status.
