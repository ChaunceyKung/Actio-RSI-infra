# micro-49 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the repair move that best restores the relationship after damage has already occurred

## Route Signal
Route here when the breach is already settled and the question is which follow-up move mends the strained relationship.

## Hard Boundary
- Not for deciding whether a remark was inappropriate in the first place; that is detecting whether a social breach occurred (the prior step).
- Not for deciding who is at fault or how blameworthy they are; that is harm and responsibility.
- Not for naming the guilt or shame the wrongdoer feels; that is a moral emotion, not the repair action.
- Not for whether to disclose a secret or how to negotiate; those involve no post-harm repair.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not pick 'do nothing / it was not technically rude' when the other person is clearly hurt; re-litigating the act is not repairing the relationship.
- Do not collapse repair into mere general niceness; the move must address the specific damage that was done.
- Do not jump to a new joint plan (reschedule, future arrangement) while ignoring the unacknowledged hurt.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering the breach-detection question ('was it rude?') instead of the repair question ('what should they do now?').
- Choosing a defensive option that argues the act was justified, instead of one that acknowledges the other's hurt.
- Choosing 'do nothing' because no apology was explicitly requested, missing that indirect or unintended harm still needs repair.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: After Xiao Wang says his mom is a cafeteria worker, a classmate sneers that he hates the cafeteria aunties; Xiao Wang goes quiet and looks hurt. What should the classmate do now to make it right? | Why: The breach is already done and someone is visibly hurt; the question is the repair move, so this skill applies.
- Case: Same cafeteria scene, but the question is only 'Did anyone say something inappropriate in this story?' | Why: Single variable flipped: this asks whether a breach OCCURRED, not how to repair it — that is detecting a social breach, handle it in the breach-detection skill, not here.

## Boundary Stress Test
- Question 'Was that line rude?' or 'Which sentence was inappropriate?' -> breach detection, not this skill.
- Question 'What should they do now to make it better?' with someone already hurt -> this skill.
- Scene shows both an offense and a proposed fix: evaluate the repair move, ignore the temptation to re-judge the original wrong.
- Question 'How bad must she feel / does she feel guilty?' -> moral emotion, not the repair action.

## Generalization Note
Covers the follow-up move after harm — apology, explanation, amends/compensation, reassurance, de-escalation — and asks which best restores the relationship. It never decides whether the first act was wrong, who is to blame, or what the actor feels.
