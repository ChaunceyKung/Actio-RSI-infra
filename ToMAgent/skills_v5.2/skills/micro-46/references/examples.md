# micro-46 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the role structure between partners

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the narrow diagnostic question is: what role, rank, authority, expertise, or status relation exists between the people, or which party is responsible for which part? It NAMES the structure; it never picks a move.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit names the structure; it does not generate a tactful response or pick a strategy. Choosing what to say or do across a power gap (defer, push back, persuade, escalate) is the power-navigation / hierarchical-communication capability.
- This unit reads rank, authority, expertise, and assigned responsibility; it does not read group belonging or stereotypes, which is group identity.
- This unit does not evaluate moral fairness except where role duties define who has authority or obligation.
- This unit does not infer loyalty, reliability, or betrayal from relationship history, and does not decide disclosure boundaries.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume the louder, older, richer, or more confident person has authority unless the scene gives a role-based reason.
- Do not collapse expertise into trustworthiness; an expert role indicates knowledge authority, not necessarily honesty or goodwill.
- Do not treat friendliness as equality when formal evaluation, permission, or supervision remains active.

## Common Failure Modes
- Jumping to advice on HOW to speak to a boss (defer, push back, persuade) instead of just naming the boss/subordinate structure - that is choosing a communication move across a power gap.
- Inferring authority from who is louder, older, richer, or more confident when the scene gives no role-based reason.
- Mistaking shared group membership (same club, culture, or team) for a rank or authority relation, which is group identity.
- Missing temporary situational authority such as a team captain, meeting chair, event host, or safety officer.
- Letting a friendly or joking tone erase a still-active evaluation, permission, or supervision role.
- Confusing role power with personal preference or popularity.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A new analyst is unsure whether the team leader or the senior deputy outranks them on assigning a task; the question asks who holds the authority to assign it. | Why: The pivot is NAMING who holds assignment authority - the role/rank structure itself. Stays in this unit.
- Case: Same office, but now the question is how the new analyst should respond when the team leader assigns them the deputy's usual task without seeming to overstep. | Why: Single variable flipped from identify to act: the structure is given and the task is now choosing a communication move across the power gap. Routes away.

## Boundary Stress Test
- A CEO and an intern play on the same weekend soccer team: identify whether the workplace hierarchy is active in the current scene or whether they are temporary teammates.
- A junior engineer corrects a senior manager about a technical bug: separate organizational rank from domain expertise.
- A teacher asks a student for help fixing a projector: the student may have technical expertise, but the teacher still holds classroom authority.
- A customer yells at a cashier: customer status gives service-role expectations, not unlimited authority over the employee.

## Generalization Note
Role structure is a social-cognitive frame for interpreting obligations, authority, deference, permission, evaluation, expertise, and assigned responsibility. Diagnose it from explicit roles and situational powers, then hand off: choosing what to say or do across the gap is the power-navigation / hierarchical-communication capability, tailoring wording to the listener is audience calibration, and group belonging or stereotype is group identity.
