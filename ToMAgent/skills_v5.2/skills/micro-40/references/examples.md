# micro-40 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
how well the expression fits the listener's knowledge, age, identity, or stance

## Route Signal
Use this capability when the scene asks whether speech is adapted to a listener's expertise, age, identity, or stance, and the options differ mainly in how understandable or acceptable they are to that listener.

## Hard Boundary
- Not about changing the listener's belief or behavior (that is the persuasion-strategy capability, micro-41).
- Not about whether the message is true, false, or misleading (that is the truth-status or misleading-expression capability, micro-37/38).
- Not about why the speaker chose the wording (that is the speaker-motivation capability, micro-39).
- Not about whether the listener is offended or embarrassed (that is the faux-pas-and-offense capability, micro-42).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not treat every tailored message as persuasion; matching a listener's level can be neutral and informative.
- Do not pick the option that aims to change the listener's decision when the question only asks which wording fits them.
- Do not infer the speaker's motive from audience-fit alone.
- Do not collapse audience calibration into social tact or group politeness.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing audience fit with persuasion strategy and choosing the most convincing option rather than the most understandable one.
- Confusing audience fit with implied meaning or hidden implication.
- Answering from the speaker's motive instead of the listener's needs.
- Overweighting tone or warmth while ignoring whether the listener's knowledge level is matched.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A clinician explains a diagnosis to a colleague in clinical terms and to a lay parent in plain everyday words; the question is which version fits the lay parent. | Why: Both versions carry the same content; the only decisive variable is which is pitched at the listener's level of understanding.
- Case: Xiao Hong wants to study computer science in another city; her parents prefer local medicine; the question is which appeal best brings her parents around. | Why: Near miss: the wording is chosen to change the parents' decision, not just to fit their knowledge. Route to the persuasion-strategy capability (micro-41).

## Boundary Stress Test
- A speaker simplifies a message to make the listener AGREE more easily: if influence is the goal, route to the persuasion-strategy capability (micro-41).
- A speaker phrases something vaguely to hide a criticism: if hidden meaning or false inference is the issue, this is not audience calibration.
- A speaker changes wording to avoid insulting an out-group: if tact or offense is central, route to the faux-pas-and-offense capability (micro-42).
- A speaker asks WHY a careful phrasing was chosen: if the answer is a social motive, route to the speaker-motivation capability (micro-39).

## Generalization Note
Generalize by checking listener-fit across knowledge, age, identity, and stance: the same message re-pitched for a different audience. The decisive variable is comprehension and acceptability fit, not influence, truth, or motive. This applies across benchmark and real-world settings, with no dependence on any fixed item format.
