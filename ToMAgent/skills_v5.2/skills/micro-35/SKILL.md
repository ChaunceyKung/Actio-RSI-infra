# Indirect Speech Act

## Use When
- Use when the question asks what social act the utterance is performing, or what the speaker 'really wants' or 'really means' by it, not just what it literally says.
- Use when an indirectly worded sentence functions as a request, refusal, warning, reminder, hint, invitation, or piece of mockery.
- Use when the answer should be an ACTION the speaker is doing to the listener (e.g. 'getting them to leave', 'asking for water'), recovered from context.
- Use when a literal statement, comment, or question is conventionally used to get the listener to do something.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the question asks only for the sentence's plain stated content; name and route to the explicit-meaning capability (Literal Meaning, micro-34).
- Do not use when the question asks what extra factual proposition is implied (e.g. a quantity or a hidden fact) rather than what act is performed; name and route to the implied-information capability (Conversational Implicature, micro-36).
- Do not use when the question is whether the statement is true, false, or a lie; name and route to the truth-status capability (Truth, Lie, and Half-Truth, micro-37).
- Do not use when the diagnostic target is WHY the speaker chose those words (politeness, face-saving, ingratiation) rather than what act they perform; name and route to the speaker-motive capability (Speaker Motivation, micro-39).

## Decision Variable
what the utterance is doing socially

## Trigger Checklist
- Is the utterance indirect, so its literal form and its real point diverge?
- Would the best answer be an ACT label such as request, refusal, warning, reminder, hint, invitation, or mockery?
- Would answering with the literal sentence alone miss what the speaker is actually doing?
- Is context needed to identify what the speaker wants the listener to DO?

## Workflow
- State the literal sentence meaning first, so you can see the form (SAY).
- Ask what the speaker is trying to get the listener to do or accept in this context (DO).
- Name the most specific speech act that fits the scene: request, refusal, warning, reminder, hint, invitation, or mockery.
- Check the act against the listener's expected response: a request expects compliance, a warning expects caution, a reminder expects recall.
- Choose the option naming that act. If the question instead asks for the plain words, an implied fact, or the truth status, stop and route out to the matching capability.

## Special Case
**A comment, need, or question used as an indirect request**: When a statement is phrased as a fact, a personal need, or a question but in context functions to make the listener act, answer with the request (or other act) it performs, not the literal content and not the speaker's emotional state.

Scene: After a long, hot highway trip George arrives at Angela's office. As Angela immediately starts pitching business ideas, George interrupts: 'Ah, I had a long and hot highway trip.'
Question: What is George really trying to express?
Answer logic: Literally George only reports a fact about his trip. But in context, interrupting Angela's pitch with this remark performs an indirect request: he is asking to rest or have a drink before talking business ('Can I drink something?' / 'Can we rest a few minutes?'). The diagnostic target is the act performed, not the literal report (which would be the explicit-meaning capability, Literal Meaning, micro-34), so the answer is the request to pause and refresh before business.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the question asks only what the sentence explicitly says, route to the explicit-meaning capability (Literal Meaning, micro-34).
- If the question asks what extra factual content is implied (a quantity, an unstated fact) rather than what act is performed, route to the implied-information capability (Conversational Implicature, micro-36).
- If truth, deception, or sincerity is the main target, route to the truth-status capability (Truth, Lie, and Half-Truth, micro-37) or the misleading-true-statement capability (Misleading and Selective Expression, micro-38).
- If the target is the social MOTIVE for wording it this way rather than the act itself, route to the speaker-motive capability (Speaker Motivation, micro-39).
- If the scene is better described as a belief, desire, or emotion inference with no utterance-act focus, route to the relevant belief, desire, or emotion capability.
