# micro-37 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
truth status of explicit content

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the diagnostic target is the factual accuracy of what was explicitly said.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit judges content-versus-fact fit only, not moral blame for lying.
- This unit does not decide whether the speaker knew the statement was false or chose it on purpose; that is the speaker-motivation capability (micro-39).
- This unit does not decide whether the listener ended up deceived; whether true-or-false wording fools the listener belongs to the misleading-and-selective-expression capability (micro-38).
- This unit does not analyze meaning implied beyond the words unless that implication is itself the proposition being checked against fact.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not label a statement false merely because it is unhelpful, evasive, or suspicious.
- Do not label a literally true statement a lie just because it omits an important fact.
- Do not use the speaker's facial expression, motive, or relationship history to change the truth value of the explicit words.

## Common Failure Modes
- Conflating factual falsity with intent to deceive.
- Treating implication as if it were explicitly stated.
- Ignoring qualifiers such as 'some,' 'all,' 'before,' 'after,' 'maybe,' or 'I think.'
- Collapsing mixed claims into a single true-or-false label without checking each component.
- Using the listener's mistaken conclusion as proof that the utterance itself was false.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Li Ming left the football at the field but thinks it is in his bag; asked where it is, he says 'It is in my bag,' and the question is whether that statement is true. | Why: In scope: the claim conflicts with the facts, so the decisive variable is the truth status of the content — and it stays FALSE even though Li Ming believed it.
- Case: Same scene, but the question is now 'Why does Li Ming say this?', choosing among deliberate lying versus honest forgetting. | Why: Out of scope: the words are already known to be false; the question is about the speaker's reason and intent, which routes to the speaker-motivation capability (micro-39).

## Boundary Stress Test
- A speaker says something false but sincerely believes it (an honest mistake): classify the content FALSE, and do NOT infer intentional lying — intent is the speaker-motivation capability's job (micro-39).
- A speaker says something kind-but-false to spare feelings (a white lie, e.g. praising an ugly hat): truth status is still FALSE; the kindness motive does not make the content true.
- A speaker says something literally true in a way that produces a false belief: classify the content TRUE, then route the misleading effect to the misleading-and-selective-expression capability (micro-38).
- A sentence contains one true clause and one false clause: mark it a half-truth / mixed-content statement rather than forcing a single true-or-false label.
- A vague statement such as 'I finished most of it' is UNSUPPORTED until the scene supplies a fact that confirms or contradicts the vague quantity.

## Generalization Note
The unit generalizes to reports, excuses, answers, denials, promises, and descriptions whenever the operation is comparing explicit asserted content against known facts — independent of why it was said or whether anyone was fooled.
