# micro-45 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
long-term reliability

## Route Signal
Use when the answer depends on whether a PERSON is dependable, loyal, honest, or consistent across a HISTORY of past interactions, not on whether one message is believable now.

## Hard Boundary
- A track record over time is required: more than one interaction, or explicit pattern words ('always', 'every time', 'again').
- The target is the person's reliability, not the truth of any single claim or message.
- A repeated pattern must outweigh one isolated event in both directions: a lone good deed does not repair a bad record, and a lone slip does not destroy a good one.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not treat a believable-sounding speaker as a trustworthy person; sounding credible now is not a track record.
- Do not confuse one good deed with a repaired reputation, or one false statement with an unreliable person.
- Do not infer trust from politeness, status, charm, or confidence alone.

## Common Failure Modes
- Collapsing trust-in-a-person into belief-in-a-statement: scoring how convincing one current message is instead of the person's history - that is source credibility.
- Treating a single false statement as proof of unreliability when it was an honest mistake or forgetting, not a pattern.
- Overweighting the most recent action and ignoring the longer pattern.
- Substituting moral approval, fairness, or blame for the reliability judgment the question actually asks.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A friend has, across two years, repeatedly kept secrets and shown up exactly when they said they would; now you must decide whether to confide something important. | Why: A multi-interaction track record of a PERSON predicts future reliability: this is trust and reputation.
- Case: A stranger gives a polished, plausible presentation; you must decide whether to believe the claims in it, and there is no shared history. | Why: Near-miss that routes OUT: this judges whether one message is believable right now, a single-instance information judgment, not a person's history - source credibility.

## Boundary Stress Test
- Person is charming and persuasive but has broken promises three times before: lower trust, because the track record outranks the charm.
- Person is blunt and unpolished but has kept every commitment for a year: reliability stays high, because dependable behavior, not likeability, sets trust.
- Person once said something false but only because they forgot, with no other slips: this is a single-statement issue, not damaged reputation - keep it with truthfulness, do not lower long-term trust.

## Generalization Note
Anchor every judgment to a multi-interaction pattern and to future dependability. If the scene gives no history and only asks whether one message rings true, the question is about information, not the person - route to source credibility.
