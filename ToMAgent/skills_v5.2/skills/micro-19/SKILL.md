# Self Preference

## Use When
- Use when the question asks what this character personally likes, wants, prefers, or would choose for themselves.
- Use when the answer is the character's OWN original desire that persists across an interruption (e.g. they wanted X, were briefly pulled away, and you must say they still want X).
- Use when the evidence is the character's own statement of liking, a stable taste, or a repeated voluntary choice they make.
- Use when the answer would NOT change if every other person in the scene were removed — it depends only on this character's desire.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the question is about what ANOTHER person likes, or about this character accommodating someone else's taste — that is modeling another person's preference (micro-20).
- Do not use when the question asks who is right, how sure the character is, or whether a source is reliable — that is certainty of a belief (micro-17) or source credibility (micro-18).
- Do not use when the answer depends on ranking competing goals, a duty, or a constraint that overrides liking — that is goal hierarchy (micro-21), commitment priority (micro-24), or goal-conflict arbitration (micro-25).
- Do not use when the question asks what would be socially wise, polite, or persuasive for the listener — that is audience calibration (micro-40) or persuasion strategy (micro-41).

## Decision Variable
the character's own preference

## Trigger Checklist
- WHOSE liking is queried? It must be the focal character's own, not their guess about someone else's.
- Is the evidence the character's own desire — a stated like, a stable taste, or a repeated voluntary choice?
- Would the answer survive deleting every other person from the scene? If yes, the variable is this character's own preference.
- Is the queried thing a LIKING (what they want), not a duty they must perform or the best move for the group?

## Workflow
- Name the focal character and confirm the question targets THAT character's own liking or wanting (not another person's, and not 'who is correct').
- Pull the character's own desire from the scene: a direct statement of liking, a stable taste, or a repeated voluntary choice. A one-off act under pressure is not yet preference.
- If the character is briefly pulled away by an interruption (an invitation, a distraction, a pause), the original desire still governs once the interruption ends — pick the option that fulfils it.
- Choose the option most aligned with that own desire; reject options that merely reflect convenience, politeness, or another person's wish.
- Exit only if the question shifts: to another person's taste, to certainty, or to a duty/goal that overrides liking (see Boundary Exit Rule).

## Special Case
**Original desire survives an interruption**: When a character has a clear own-desire and is briefly pulled away by an unrelated interruption (an invitation, a distraction, a pause), the action after the interruption still serves the ORIGINAL desire. Do not let the interruption overwrite the preference.

Scene: Susan bakes cookies because she wants to eat them. Before she can, a friend invites her to go swimming, so she sets the cookies aside and goes.
Question: What does Susan do after she comes home from swimming?
Answer logic: Susan's own desire was to eat the cookies she baked; swimming was an unrelated interruption, not a step taken to get the cookies. So once she is home she eats her cookies. The interruption does not change her preference.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the question turns to what SOMEONE ELSE likes, or to this character choosing FOR another person to suit that person's taste, exit to the other-person preference capability — modeling what another person wants (micro-20).
- If the question is about confidence, rumor, evidence, or how reliable a source is, exit to certainty of a belief (micro-17) or source credibility (micro-18).
- If the character acts AGAINST their own liking because of a duty, fear, or competing aim, exit to commitment priority (micro-24) or goal-conflict arbitration (micro-25) — the deciding variable is which goal wins, not what they like.
- If the answer is about what is polite, persuasive, or best for the listener, exit to audience calibration (micro-40) or persuasion strategy (micro-41).
