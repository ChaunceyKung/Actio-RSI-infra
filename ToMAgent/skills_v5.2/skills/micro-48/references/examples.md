# micro-48 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
whether disclosing this information to this audience crosses its privacy/secrecy boundary

## Route Signal
Route here when the scene names a private fact, secret, or surprise and the live question is whether telling a particular person or group was allowed.

## Hard Boundary
- Does not judge general rudeness or tactlessness when no private information is being disclosed; that is broader social tact / faux pas.
- Does not handle what to do after a secret is already out; apology and trust repair is relationship repair.
- Does not cover softening truth to protect feelings about someone's own thing with no third-party secret; that is a kind white lie.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not call withholding a secret a lie; keeping entrusted information private is correct conduct, not deception.
- Do not assume a positive or harmless-sounding detail is safe to share; a flattering fact (an award, a happy surprise) can still be private and off-limits.
- Do not jump straight to apology because a disclosure feels awkward; first decide whether the boundary was actually crossed.

## Common Failure Modes
- Treating any awkward remark as a privacy issue when it is really general tactlessness with no secret involved (should be general social tact / faux pas).
- Marking the holder who KEEPS the secret as the offender, instead of the one who LEAKS it.
- Assuming 'positive news is fine to share' and missing that a flattering detail can still be private.
- Sliding into apology/repair logic when the only question is whether to disclose.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A friend who was told a family's private trouble in confidence repeats it to a group of curious classmates. | Why: A specific entrusted secret is disclosed to people outside the entitled circle, so the disclosure boundary decides it; this is this unit.
- Case: Identical confidence, but the friend deflects the same curious classmates and says nothing about the family. | Why: Same private fact, withheld from outsiders; the holder acts correctly, which is the appropriate side of the same disclosure boundary (still this unit).
- Case: At a party someone loudly brags about a promotion others were passed over for; nothing secret is revealed, the comment is just tactless. | Why: The awkwardness is general insensitivity with no private information leaked, so it is broader social tact / faux pas, not this unit.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the audience is polite and curious but not entitled to know, telling them still crosses the boundary.
- If the detail is flattering or happy rather than embarrassing, it can still be private and off-limits.
- If the secret is already public knowledge, repeating it crosses no boundary and this unit no longer applies.

## Generalization Note
The judgment is constant across friendships, family, work, and online life: who is entitled to know this item, and did the speaker tell someone outside that circle. It does not extend to general rudeness, to repairing a breach, or to softening truth about someone's own thing.
