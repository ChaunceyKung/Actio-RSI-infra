# Goal Hierarchy

## Use When
- Use when one stated goal is an instrument or step taken in order to reach another goal, and you must say which is the end and which is the means.
- Use when a character does a small task only because it unlocks something they wanted all along (e.g., helps a neighbor to earn money, then uses the money for their original aim).
- Use when the question is about the level or nesting of goals (long-term aim vs. short-term step vs. local action), not about which of two rival goals the character picks.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when two goals COMPETE and the question is which one wins or is weighted higher with no nesting between them; that is free weighing of rival goals (Goal-Conflict Arbitration, micro-25).
- Do not use when a binding prior promise, ongoing task, or schedule decides what gets done first; that is Commitment Priority (micro-24).
- Do not use when the question asks what the character will physically do next from their goal; that is Action Prediction (micro-23).
- Do not use when the issue is inferring the immediate purpose behind one present behavior; that is Intention Recognition (micro-22).
- Do not use when the issue is reading what the character or another person likes or wants (self- or other-preference, micro-19/micro-20).

## Decision Variable
the means-end (nesting) relation among goals: which goal is superordinate (the end) and which is subordinate (a step serving it)

## Trigger Checklist
- Two or more goals, plans, or aims are present and one is performed in order to obtain another.
- You can phrase the link as 'does A so that B' or 'A is a step toward B'.
- The goals do not compete for the same slot; completing the subgoal advances, not blocks, the end goal.
- The question is about level/structure (end vs. step), not about which rival goal is chosen or what happens next.

## Workflow
- List every stated goal, plan, or action and the character's original want.
- For each pair, test 'is A done in order to reach B?' If yes, A is the subordinate (means) and B is the superordinate (end).
- Trace the full chain to the top: the original want the character is ultimately trying to satisfy is the end goal.
- Map each item as end goal, intermediate means, or local step. Answer with that structure, or with the action that shows the character resuming the END goal after a subgoal is done.

## Special Case
**Detour subgoal that unlocks the original want**: When a character pauses an original want to do a task that supplies the means (money, permission, an item) for that same want, the task is a subordinate step; the original want is still the end goal and is resumed once the means is obtained.

Scene: Tom finds money and wants to buy things with it. An aunt offers him money if he helps pick apples, so he stays to pick apples.
Question: What does Tom do after he helps the aunt pick apples?
Answer logic: Picking apples is a subordinate step taken only to earn money; buying things was, and remains, the superordinate end goal. So once the subgoal is done, Tom goes to spend the money on the things he wanted, not back to the aunt's chores or off to play.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to the free-weighing-of-rival-goals capability if two goals compete for the same slot and the question is which one wins or matters more (Goal-Conflict Arbitration, micro-25).
- Exit to the binding-commitment capability if a prior promise, ongoing task, or schedule is what decides the order of action (Commitment Priority, micro-24).
- Exit to the next-action-prediction capability if the scene asks what the character will physically do next (Action Prediction, micro-23).
- Exit to the immediate-purpose-of-a-behavior capability if the issue is inferring why one current behavior is happening (Intention Recognition, micro-22).
- Exit to the desire/preference-reading capability if the issue is what the character's own or another person's likes or wants are (self-preference micro-19 / other-preference micro-20).
