# micro-22 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the immediate purpose behind the behavior

## Route Signal
Use this capability when an already-observed behavior is the evidence and the diagnostic question is WHY that behavior is happening - the immediate purpose it serves right now. If the question instead asks what the character does next, route to the next-action prediction capability (micro-23).

## Hard Boundary
- This unit explains a behavior that has already occurred; it does NOT forecast the next behavior. 'Why does she smile?' is in scope; 'what will she do now?' routes to the next-action prediction capability (micro-23).
- This unit reads the purpose of a behavior or nonverbal cue; if the cue is spoken words whose social action must be decoded, route to the indirect-speech-act capability (micro-35).
- This unit outputs an immediate intention; it does not resolve stable preferences, personality traits, or long-term values.
- This unit may use beliefs and goals as evidence, but its output is a purpose - not a belief state or a goal ranking.
- This unit applies only when the behavior is plausibly intentional or socially meaningful.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not explain the cue using what the narrator or a bystander knows if the actor does not know it; reason strictly from the actor's vantage point.
- Do not slide from the cue to a predicted next action; first answer what the cue is FOR. If you catch yourself describing a later move, you have crossed into the next-action prediction capability (micro-23).
- Do not read every smile, glance, or pause as deception, romance, or generic friendliness; use the cue's direction, target, and the audience present to pin the specific purpose.
- Do not pick a broad motive ('to be polite', 'to be nice') when the scene supports a sharper, concrete purpose.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with a predicted next action instead of the purpose of the current behavior (the core confusion with the next-action prediction capability).
- Explaining the behavior from the narrator's omniscient knowledge rather than the actor's perspective.
- Ignoring the target or direction of the cue - who the gaze, nudge, or signal is aimed at.
- Choosing a vague motive like 'to be friendly' when a more specific immediate purpose is supported by the cue's form.
- Confusing a stable preference with a momentary intention.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Xiao Hong glances at the swing, then smiles at Xiao Fang, who nods. Question: 'Why does Xiao Hong smile at Xiao Fang?' | Why: The behavior has already happened and the question word is WHY: the answer is the cue's immediate purpose - to suggest they go make a new friend together. In scope for this unit.
- Case: Zhiwei notices an uninvited classmate listening and glances at Xiaolan. Question: 'After Zhiwei notices him, what is his next action?' | Why: Same kind of glance cue, but the question asks for the NEXT move (suggest changing the topic or place). That routes to the next-action prediction capability - what the character is most likely to do next (micro-23) - because the answer is a future behavior, not the purpose of the glance.

## Boundary Stress Test
- A person glances at the door during a meeting: decide whether the glance is checking the time, signaling a wish to leave, noticing an arrival, or avoiding eye contact - using timing and audience, and stopping at the purpose without predicting their next move.
- A child hides a broken toy behind their back: name the immediate intention (to conceal the damage) separately from any later action they might take - the later action belongs to the next-action prediction capability (micro-23).
- Someone says 'Ah, I had a long, hot drive' on arriving: this is a spoken hint, so its meaning ('may I have a drink?') is recovered by the indirect-speech-act capability (micro-35), not by reading a nonverbal cue.
- Someone waves across a room: identify whether the wave greets, summons, warns, or confirms attention before any follow-up is predicted.

## Generalization Note
The unit generalizes across nonverbal and behavioral cues: an observed cue, plus the actor's perspective, plus scene context yields an immediate social purpose. The constant test is direction of time - this unit looks backward to explain a behavior already shown, whereas the next-action prediction capability (micro-23) looks forward to the next move.
