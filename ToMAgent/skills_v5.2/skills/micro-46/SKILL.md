# Power, Status, and Role

## Use When
- Use when the question asks who has authority, rank, expertise, or role-based control in an interaction, or which party is responsible for which part of a task.
- Use when labels such as teacher/student, manager/employee, doctor/patient, expert/novice, host/guest, parent/child, officer/citizen, or senior/junior shape how the scene is interpreted.
- Use when the answer depends on whether the relation is hierarchical, peer-to-peer, advisory, supervisory, service-based, or dependency-based.
- Use when a social norm follows from position or role rather than from personal liking, fairness, trust history, or group identity.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use to pick what someone should SAY or DO given a power gap (comply, defer, push back, escalate, persuade, apologize); this unit only NAMES the structure - acting within it is choosing a communication move across a power gap, the power-navigation / hierarchical-communication capability.
- Do not use when the variable is whether the relation is in-group/out-group, stereotype, belonging, or shared identity membership rather than rank or authority; that is group-identity reasoning.
- Do not use when the task is adapting wording to a listener's level or background; that is audience calibration.
- Do not use when the main variable is whether someone is reliable or honest over time; use trust and reputation reasoning.
- Do not use when the main variable is whether a resource split, repayment, or burden is fair; use fairness and reciprocity reasoning.
- Do not use when the main variable is whether information should be kept private or disclosed; use privacy, secrets, and boundaries reasoning.

## Decision Variable
the role structure between partners

## Trigger Checklist
- Are there explicit role labels such as boss, teacher, doctor, client, officer, parent, expert, trainee, host, or guest?
- Does one person have formal authority to evaluate, permit, assign, discipline, grade, hire, pay, treat, or enforce?
- Does one person have recognized expertise or gatekeeping power even without formal rank?
- Does the question ask how status, rank, role obligation, or institutional position changes interpretation?
- Would the answer remain mostly the same if personal friendship, liking, or prior trust history were removed?

## Workflow
- Confirm the question is asking WHO occupies WHICH role or WHO holds authority/responsibility, not what anyone should say or do. If it asks for a move (persuade, defer, push back, comply, apologize), stop and hand off after step 5.
- List the interaction partners and every explicit title, role, institutional setting, or assigned task responsibility in the scene (e.g. team leader, the technical part, the market analysis).
- Identify the source of asymmetry for each pair: formal authority, recognized expertise, dependency, seniority, service role, caregiving role, or temporary situational control (chair, host, captain).
- Classify the relation as hierarchical, expert-novice, caregiver-dependent, service-provider/client, host-guest, peer, or unclear/mixed. Use only scene evidence; do not infer authority from who is louder, older, richer, or more confident.
- Separate role from behavior quality and from familiarity: a rude manager is still a manager, a kind teacher is still an evaluator, and a friendly tone does not suspend evaluation, permission, or supervision power.
- State only the role-based relation and the norm it activates (deference, duty of care, evaluation power, permission control, confidentiality expectation, obligation to follow instructions). Do NOT draft a reply or pick a tactic - that is choosing a communication move across a power gap, a separate task.

## Special Case
**Informal familiarity does not erase formal role**: When people are friendly or familiar but one still holds institutional authority or expert responsibility, preserve the formal role unless the scene explicitly suspends it.

Scene: A student jokes casually with her professor after class, then asks whether she can submit the assignment late.
Question: What role structure matters here?
Answer logic: Even though the tone is friendly, the professor still has evaluator and permission-granting authority over the student. The relevant structure is teacher/student with formal academic authority.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the task asks what the lower- or higher-status person should say or do given the power gap (defer, push back, escalate, persuade, comply), name the role here, then exit to choosing a communication move across a power gap.
- If the task is about wording the message to fit the listener's level or background, exit to audience calibration.
- If the task asks whether an identity category, stereotype, or group belonging (same club, culture, team) explains interpretation rather than rank, exit to group identity.
- If the task asks whether the person deserves trust because of past actions, exit to trust and reputation reasoning.
- If the task asks whether someone took advantage, owed repayment, or split benefits unfairly, exit to fairness and reciprocity reasoning.
- If the task asks whether a secret, medical fact, salary, grade, or personal detail may be shared, exit to privacy, secrets, and boundaries reasoning.
