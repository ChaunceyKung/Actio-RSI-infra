# Privacy, Secrets, and Boundaries

## Use When
- Use when the scene contains a specific item of private, secret, or sensitive information (a confided secret, a medical or family matter, a planned surprise, a confidential detail) and the question turns on whether saying it was appropriate.
- Use when someone was told or trusted to keep something, and you must judge whether disclosing it crossed a line or whether keeping it quiet was the right call.
- Use when the diagnostic pivot is the audience: this fact is fine for one circle but off-limits to another, and the question is whether the speaker leaked it to people not entitled to know.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the remark is awkward or rude for reasons OTHER than disclosing private information (bragging, tactlessness, a hurtful but public comment); judging whether a remark is socially inappropriate in general is broader social tact / faux pas.
- Do not use when the secret has already been let out and the question is what to do next; making amends, apologizing, and rebuilding trust after a breach is relationship repair.
- Do not use when the truth is being softened or withheld to spare someone's feelings about THEIR OWN thing (praising an ugly hat, an average painting); that is a kind white lie / protective concealment, not a privacy boundary.
- Do not use when the point is what a remark hints at beyond its literal words; reading implied meaning is conversational implicature.

## Decision Variable
whether disclosing this information to this audience crosses its privacy/secrecy boundary

## Trigger Checklist
- Is there a concrete item of information that is private, secret, confidential, or a planned surprise?
- Did someone designate it as not-to-be-told, or is it private by default (health, family trouble, someone else's personal life)?
- Does the question hinge on whether telling a particular audience was appropriate, rather than on apology, role/status, or general rudeness?
- Is the boundary still intact at decision time (the choice to tell or withhold is the live issue), not already breached?

## Workflow
- 1. Name the specific item of information at stake (the secret, surprise, diagnosis, family matter, confidential fact).
- 2. Establish its boundary: who is entitled to know it? Was it explicitly entrusted ('do not tell X'), private by default, or already public?
- 3. Identify the actual audience the speaker disclosed it to (or would disclose it to) and check whether that audience is inside or outside the entitled circle.
- 4. Apply the rule: telling it to someone OUTSIDE the entitled circle is a breach / inappropriate; keeping it within that circle, or deflecting outsiders, is appropriate. A flattering or harmless-seeming detail can still be off-limits.
- 5. If asked WHY a disclosure was wrong, the answer is that the speaker revealed information that was private or had been entrusted as secret to people who were not supposed to hear it.

## Special Case
**Same Information, Disclose vs Withhold**: When two scenes carry the identical private fact and differ only in whether the holder spoke it to outsiders, the disclosure is the faux pas and the withholding is correct conduct. The wrongness lives in the act of telling people not entitled, not in the fact itself.

Scene: Xiao Gang tells Xiao Ming his mother is ill and the family wants it kept private; Xiao Ming agrees. Later, when curious classmates ask, Xiao Ming says 'Everything is normal at Xiao Gang's house.'
Question: Did anyone say something inappropriate?
Answer logic: No one did. The information was entrusted as private, and Xiao Ming kept it inside the entitled circle by deflecting outsiders. Contrast the matched scene where the confidant instead repeats the family secret to classmates: there the disclosure to non-entitled people is the breach.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to broader social tact / faux pas if the remark is inappropriate for reasons other than leaking private info, e.g. boasting, insensitivity, or a hurtful comment about something already public.
- Exit to relationship repair if the secret is already out and the question is how to apologize, explain, or rebuild trust.
- Exit to kind white lies / protective concealment if truth is being softened to spare someone's feelings about their own work or gift, with no third-party secret involved.
- Exit to conversational implicature if the task is to decode what a remark implies rather than whether private information should be disclosed.
