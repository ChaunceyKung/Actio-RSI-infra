# micro-50 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
what impression of THE SPEAKER the speaker wants others to form, and how they shape self-information to get it

## Route Signal
Route here when the scene's pivot is how the SPEAKER shapes their own self-information (order, emphasis, omission, self-description) to control the impression others form of THEM.

## Hard Boundary
- Emotion display rules cover hiding or softening one FEELING to be polite or to protect another person; this unit covers managing the speaker's OVERALL self-image. A kind lie to spare someone is display rules even if an option says 'to build her own good image'.
- Speaking motive asks why a single sentence is phrased a certain way; this unit asks what self-image the speaker is building across what they reveal.
- Persuasion is one-way influence on someone's decision about an external claim; this unit is about the speaker's own projected identity, not winning a request.
- Privacy asks whether information may be shared at all; this unit assumes presentation is happening and asks how it is shaped.
- Repair fixes harm after a social break; this unit is about image before or apart from any offense.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not equate impression management with lying; truthful ordering and framing can be self-presentation.
- Do not pick this unit just because an answer option mentions 'image', 'reputation', or 'looking good'; if the real driver is sparing another's feelings, those are wrong distractors.
- Do not collapse self-presentation into secrecy; the variable is the intended impression, not whether a fact is concealed.

## Common Failure Modes
- Choosing self-image on a kind-lie scene because an option says 'to build a good image', when the real driver is protecting the other person's feelings.
- Reading any selective disclosure as deception rather than truthful framing for image.
- Confusing managing the speaker's OWN image with managing another person's emotion.
- Missing that disclosure ORDER alone (what is led with) can be the diagnostic clue for self-presentation.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A job candidate leads her self-introduction with team-leadership experience and saves the recent employment gap for later. | Why: She is ordering her own self-information to shape how competent she looks; the target managed is HER image, so this is self-presentation.
- Case: Aunt Wang hates the dish but tells the cook it is delicious; one option says she does it to build her good image in the community. | Why: The driver is sparing the cook's feelings, not her own standing, so this is emotion display rules; the 'good image' option is a self-presentation distractor that is wrong.

## Boundary Stress Test
- Swap who is protected: if the lie shields ANOTHER person's feelings it is display rules; if it builds the speaker's own standing it is self-presentation.
- Reorder the same true facts: if the impression of the speaker changes, this unit applies; if nothing about self-image is at stake, it does not.
- If the scene's goal is to get someone to agree to an external request, the center of gravity is persuasion, not self-presentation.

## Generalization Note
This unit covers bios, introductions, interviews, first impressions, profiles, selective disclosure, and ordering or framing of self-information whenever the primary variable is the impression others should form of the speaker.
