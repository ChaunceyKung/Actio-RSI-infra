# micro-55 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
how common or typical the category, outcome, or reaction is by default, used as a prior with no specific sample observed

## Route Signal
Use this unit when likelihood turns on a prior: commonness, category frequency, ordinary prevalence, or the default reaction to a familiar event, with no specific sample observed to update on.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit judges category or reaction likelihood from a prior, not the approximate range of a quantifier word.
- This unit uses a standing base rate, not revision on a freshly observed sample; if a count was just seen and must move the estimate, that is the updating neighbor, not this unit.
- This unit does not assign causation, blame, responsibility, or moral fault.
- This unit does not infer hidden beliefs or intentions unless they only serve as evidence for a likelihood comparison.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not pick the option that sounds most vivid, narratively interesting, or stereotype-matching while ignoring a stated base rate.
- Do not treat typicality as certainty; the common case can still be wrong for a particular individual.
- Do not silently turn a prior into a posterior: if the scene reports an observed sample, you have left this unit.

## Common Failure Modes
- Base-rate neglect: choosing a rare category because a vivid description resembles it.
- Overcorrection: clinging to the base rate even when the scene gives explicitly diagnostic individuating evidence.
- Treating an observed sample as if it were a base rate, or vice versa, instead of updating when data was actually seen.
- Confusing frequency with cause: assuming the commonest factor must have caused the event.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A clinic usually sees flu and cold cases about equally. With no patients yet examined today, which is more likely to walk in next? | Why: Pure prior: the standing base rate is the only cue and nothing has been observed, so this is base-rate reasoning, this unit.
- Case: Same clinic, but today 8 of the first 10 patients tested flu-positive; should the flu estimate be revised upward? | Why: A specific sample was just observed and must move the estimate. Single variable changed (observed data now exists), and that routes to the quantity-update-after-observation skill, not here.
- Case: A teacher says most students submitted the form; what approximate share is that? | Why: The task is decoding the quantifier word most, not comparing category likelihoods, so it routes to the vague-quantity interpretation skill.

## Boundary Stress Test
- Vivid but non-diagnostic description plus a strong stated base rate: keep the base rate central (stay in this unit).
- A random draw from a population with known proportions: use those proportions (this unit).
- A new sample such as 8 of 10 observed cases is reported: you are now updating on data, so hand off to the observed-sample updating skill.
- A character directly saw the answer: perception settles it; do not override it with typicality.

## Generalization Note
Prior-likelihood reasoning is not only numeric puzzles: the typical emotional reaction to an ordinary event, expected social roles, everyday risk, group membership, and likely behavior all rest on base rate or typicality when direct evidence is incomplete.
