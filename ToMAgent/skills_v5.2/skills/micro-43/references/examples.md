# micro-43 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
blameworthiness for harm (does this person deserve fault, given cause plus intent or negligence)

## Route Signal
Route here when the prompt asks whether a person DESERVES BLAME for a harm, given how it was caused and whether it was intended or careless, not merely what caused it.

## Hard Boundary
- Do not route here when the question only asks WHY an outcome occurred or WHAT caused it; explaining a cause without judging fault is causal attribution.
- Do not route here for rudeness or embarrassment with no real harm (faux pas/tact), and do not route here for what to do after harm to repair the relationship (apology/repair).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not equate cause with blame: a person can directly cause a harm yet not be blameworthy if it was an unforeseeable accident.
- Do not infer blame from the outcome alone: that harm occurred does not prove the actor intended it or was careless.
- Do not treat a mere awkward or rude remark as harm; offense without damage is a tact question, not a blame question.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering a pure WHY-did-it-happen question with a blame verdict when the prompt only wanted the cause (cause-vs-blame confusion).
- Assigning blame from outcome alone, ignoring that the act was an unintended, unforeseeable accident.
- Treating only deliberate harm as blameworthy and overlooking negligence or recklessness as a source of fault.
- Reading social embarrassment or a rude remark as a harm-and-blame case when no real harm occurred.
- Letting the actor off as indifferent (not my concern) when they did cause the harm and bear responsibility for it.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A waitress accidentally knocks coffee onto a customer's documents and ruins them; the question asks whether she is at fault and what she feels about it. | Why: The contested point is responsibility and blame for a real harm she caused, weighed against the lack of intent. Routes HERE (harm and responsibility).
- Case: A boy receives a bike for his birthday and is disappointed; the question asks only WHY he feels that way, not whether anyone is at fault. | Why: No harm and no fault is judged; the question only seeks the cause of an emotion. Routes to causal attribution, not here. Single variable that flips it: the question asks the cause, not the blame.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the question only asks why the outcome happened, answer with the cause and do not deliver a blame verdict; route to causal attribution.
- If there is no actual harm and the prompt asks whether a remark was rude or embarrassing, route to faux pas and social tact.
- If the actor clearly caused the harm but could not have foreseen or prevented it, the verdict is low or no blame even though they are the cause.
- If the task is how the actor should apologize or make amends, route to relationship repair.

## Generalization Note
This unit isolates ONE variable: blameworthiness for harm, the product of causation plus culpability (intent or negligence). It stays separate from naming a cause without judging fault (causal attribution), from rudeness without harm (faux pas/tact), from merely labeling the emotion felt (moral emotions), and from how to repair the relationship afterward (apology/repair).
