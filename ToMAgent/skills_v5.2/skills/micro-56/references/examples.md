# micro-56 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the cause of the result

## Route Signal
Use this unit when the prompt asks WHY an outcome happened or WHAT caused, produced, or led to it, especially a surprising result where someone 'should feel X but feels Y'. The answer is the explaining factor, not a verdict on who is to blame.

## Hard Boundary
- Naming a cause is not assigning blame. The cause may be an accident, a hidden background fact, or no one's fault at all.
- A cause can be an event, condition, unmet expectation, OR a person's action: naming who DID the thing that produced the outcome is still causal attribution and stays in this unit. Only a question asking who is at FAULT, guilty, responsible, should apologize, or said the wrong thing exits to the harm-and-responsibility neighbor.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not slide from 'what caused it' to 'who is the bad / guilty person.'
- Do not pick the person nearest the outcome when the real cause is a hidden condition or unmet expectation.
- Do not infer the actor's intention or purpose when the question only asks for the cause of a result.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering a 'why is he disappointed' question with a blame target instead of the unmet-expectation cause (he wanted a computer, not a bicycle).
- Picking a surface detail in the scene over the factor that actually explains the surprising reaction.
- Treating an accidental or no-fault cause as wrong because it is not blameworthy.
- Mistaking a 'who is at fault / who should apologize / which sentence was inappropriate' question for a cause question and answering with a cause anyway.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A composition-contest winner casually tells the disappointed loser his entry was 'just a bit regrettable.' Question: why does the loser feel hurt? | Why: Asks for the CAUSE of the feeling (the careless remark plus his crushed hope of winning). Stays in this unit.
- Case: Same scene. Question: which sentence was inappropriate / who said the wrong thing? | Why: Single variable flipped from cause to social fault. This now asks who is at fault, so it routes to the harm-and-responsibility / faux-pas neighbor, not here.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If a person caused harm by accident, can you still name the cause without declaring them guilty?
- When the scene gives a surprising emotion, can you find the hidden condition that explains it rather than a surface detail?
- If the wording sounds like blame ('who should apologize'), check: does it ask for the cause, or for fault? If fault, exit this unit.

## Generalization Note
Covers causal explanation across physical, social, and event-based scenes, but only when the answer variable is the cause that produced the outcome, not blame, intention, or responsibility.
