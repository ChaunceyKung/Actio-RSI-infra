# Persuasion Strategy

## Use When
- Use when the question asks how a speaker persuades or convinces another person to think, feel, or act differently.
- Use when the decisive variable is which appeal, argument, framing, evidence, request, or incentive will best move the listener.
- Use when the influence is one-way: one person trying to change another, not a mutual trade.
- Use when the listener already resists or disagrees and the task is to overcome that resistance.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the question is WHY the speaker said something (politeness, face-saving, humor); that is the speaker-motivation capability (micro-39).
- Do not use when the task is to fit wording to the listener's age, expertise, or identity WITHOUT trying to change them; that is the audience-calibration capability (micro-40).
- Do not use when both sides make offers or trade concessions toward a shared plan; that is the negotiation capability (micro-51).
- Do not use when the question is whether speech is rude, offensive, or a faux pas; that is the faux-pas-and-offense capability (micro-42).
- Do not use when the question is who caused harm or deserves blame; that is the harm-and-responsibility capability (micro-43).

## Decision Variable
the one-way influence strategy directed at the listener

## Trigger Checklist
- Does the question ask how to persuade, convince, or get someone to change their mind, feeling, or action?
- Is the listener resisting or holding a different view that the speaker wants to move?
- Is the influence one-way, with no reciprocal offer or concession from the listener?
- Would the best answer name an appeal — reason, evidence, emotion, what the listener values, authority, social proof, or an incentive — aimed at the listener?

## Workflow
- Identify the target listener, their current resistance, and the change the speaker wants.
- Confirm the influence is one-way (no mutual concessions) and the goal is to change the listener, not to explain a motive or merely fit their level.
- Read each option as a candidate tactic; ask which one most directly addresses THIS listener's objection or values.
- Prefer the appeal tied to what this specific listener cares about over a generic or merely informative statement.
- If the scene involves reciprocal offers, exit to the negotiation capability; if it only asks why the speaker spoke or how to fit their level, exit to those capabilities.

## Special Case
**Persuasion versus negotiation**: If both sides make offers, trade concessions, or settle on a shared plan, route away from this capability. Persuasion strategy is one person changing another person's response, not mutual bargaining. A counter-offer from the listener is the signal that it has become negotiation.

Scene: Xiao Hong, a 6-year-old, really wants to go to the amusement park; her dad objects that it is too noisy. The question is which appeal she should use to bring him around. The best option is to tell him she has not been in a long time and that this is a very special wish she truly cares about.
Question: How does Xiao Hong persuade her dad?
Answer logic: This is persuasion strategy: a one-way influence tactic aimed at changing the dad's decision. The winning appeal speaks to what will move him — the personal importance of the wish — rather than offering a trade or settling on a different outing.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to the speaker-motivation capability — why the speaker spoke that way (micro-39) — if the task is to infer the motive rather than to change the listener.
- Exit to the audience-calibration capability — fitting wording to the listener's knowledge, identity, or comfort (micro-40) — if the goal is comprehension fit, not influence.
- Exit to the negotiation capability — compromise, trades, and mutually acceptable plans (micro-51) — if the interaction contains reciprocal concessions, a counter-offer, or shared decision-making.
- Exit to the faux-pas-and-offense capability (micro-42) or the harm-and-responsibility capability (micro-43) if the key judgment is tact, offense, harm, or blame rather than influence.
