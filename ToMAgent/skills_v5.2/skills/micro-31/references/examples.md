# micro-31 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
actual outcome versus salient alternative outcome

## Route Signal
Route here when the scene asks for regret, relief, disappointment, or pleasant surprise based on a better-or-worse alternative outcome.

## Hard Boundary
- The deciding cue must be a salient alternative outcome the character contrasts with reality; without that contrast, the basic-emotion-from-an-event capability handles the emotion (micro-26).
- If guilt, shame, pride, anger, or gratitude depends on responsibility or a norm violation, route to the responsibility/norm-appraisal capability even when the outcome was bad (micro-30).
- The unit reads the felt emotion (regret, relief, disappointment, pleasant surprise); how the character shows or hides it belongs to the emotional-display-rules capability (micro-29), and how they cope with or reframe it belongs to the emotion-regulation capability (micro-33).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not label any bad outcome as regret by default.
- Do not confuse disappointment with simple sadness.
- Do not infer relief unless the character is comparing the actual outcome with a worse possible outcome.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with a generic positive or negative emotion instead of the counterfactual one.
- Using blame or morality when the scene is only about outcome comparison.
- Missing implied alternatives and treating the scene as plain success or failure.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A commuter just misses her bus, then hears that the bus she missed was in a minor crash, and feels relief. | Why: In scope. The emotion (relief) exists only because the actual outcome is weighed against the worse alternative she narrowly avoided; missing the bus by itself would be mildly annoying. The reality-versus-alternative contrast is the single decisive variable.
- Case: The same commuter just misses her bus and waits for the next one, with no information about what happened to the bus she missed. | Why: Out of scope. With no salient alternative being weighed, the feeling is plain annoyance at a direct event, so this routes to the basic-emotion-from-an-event capability (micro-26); the only variable that changed is whether a salient alternative is in play.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the character feels bad because they were careless, ask whether blame is driving the answer; if yes, exit to moral emotion.
- If the character is simply sad about a loss with no meaningful alternative, do not use this unit.
- If the scene mentions 'should have' but the question is really about shame or guilt, do not force a counterfactual reading.

## Generalization Note
This unit is about emotional contrast, not outcome quality alone: use it only when the comparison with an alternative outcome changes the emotion label.
