# Relationship Repair and Apology

## Use When
- Use when the damage has ALREADY happened (someone is hurt, offended, excluded, let down, or strained) and the question asks what to do NEXT to mend it.
- Use when the answer must choose among repair moves: apologize, explain, make amends or compensate, reassure, or de-escalate the tension.
- Use when the scene shows a visible aftermath (the other person is distant, cold, hurt, or pulling away) and the task is the actor's follow-up response.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the question is whether the original act WAS a breach at all ('Did someone say something inappropriate? Which line was rude?'); detecting the breach is the prior step, handle it in the faux-pas / social-breach-detection skill.
- Do not use when the question is WHO caused the harm or whether they are blameworthy; that is the harm-and-responsibility skill.
- Do not use when the question is what the wrongdoer FEELS (guilt, remorse, shame); naming the moral emotion is a different skill from choosing the repair action.
- Do not use when the issue is whether to disclose or keep a secret, or how to negotiate a joint plan, with no relationship yet damaged.

## Decision Variable
the repair move that best restores the relationship after damage has already occurred

## Trigger Checklist
- Has damage already landed (someone is hurt, offended, excluded, disappointed, or visibly strained)?
- Does the question ask 'what should they do NOW / next' rather than 'was that wrong / who is at fault'?
- Are the answer options repair actions (apologize, explain, make amends, reassure, smooth over) rather than verdicts?
- Is the target the relationship's restoration, not the verdict on the act or the feeling inside the actor?

## Workflow
- Gate 1 - confirm the question is about the AFTERMATH. If it asks whether the act was rude/a breach, who is to blame, or what the actor feels, this is not the repair skill; route to breach-detection, harm-and-responsibility, or moral-emotion respectively.
- Gate 2 - confirm damage actually occurred. If nobody is yet hurt or strained (only a worry about how a remark might land), there is nothing to repair yet.
- Name the damage type and what the injured party needs: hurt feelings need acknowledgment and reassurance; a broken promise or material loss needs amends or compensation; a misunderstanding needs explanation; rising conflict needs de-escalation.
- Match the repair move to that need; favor a move that ACKNOWLEDGES the other person's experience over one that defends or excuses the actor.
- Pick the least costly move that would credibly restore trust, and reject options that deny the harm, re-litigate whether it was wrong, or skip straight to a new plan without addressing the strain.

## Special Case
**Silent or indirect harm still needs repair**: Repair can be the right answer even when no apology was demanded and the harm was unintended or indirect. A quiet acknowledgment, reassurance, or small make-good can be correct; 'do nothing because no one asked' is a trap.

Scene: Mina forgets her friend's birthday dinner. No one confronts her, but afterward the friend is noticeably distant and cool toward her.
Question: What should Mina do next to repair things?
Answer logic: The breach is already settled — forgetting was the lapse — so the task is not to judge that lapse but to choose the repair. The friend's coldness signals hurt feelings, so Mina should acknowledge the oversight, apologize sincerely, and offer a small make-good (reschedule, a gesture). Doing nothing because no apology was demanded misses the needed repair.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the question is whether the act itself was rude, inappropriate, or a breach, exit to the skill that detects whether a social breach occurred.
- If the question is who caused the harm or whether they are blameworthy, exit to the harm-and-responsibility skill.
- If the question is what the wrongdoer feels inside (guilt, shame, remorse), exit to the moral-emotion skill.
- If no damage has occurred yet, or the issue is disclosure or negotiation with no strain, do not force repair reasoning.
