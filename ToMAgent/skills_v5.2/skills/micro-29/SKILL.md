# Emotional Disguise and Display Rules

## Use When
- Use when the question asks why a character smiles, stays calm, hides disappointment, acts pleased, or otherwise manages emotional expression for politeness, professionalism, safety, ceremony, or role expectations.
- Use when the key issue is not merely what the person feels, but what social rule determines what they should show.
- Use when outward emotion is shaped by audience, setting, relationship, status, or occasion.
- Use when explaining a deliberate or learned expression-control strategy such as masking, softening, exaggerating, neutralizing, or substituting an emotion.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the task only asks to NAME the true feeling behind a contrary display, without asking why the display is socially controlled; that is the detect-the-mismatch capability - identifying the hidden inner feeling (micro-28).
- Do not use when the character actually CHANGES the inner feeling - reappraising the situation, calming down, or shifting attention - rather than just controlling the outward expression; that is the emotion-regulation capability (micro-33).
- Do not use when the emotion itself is surprising or nonstandard but there is no rule-based expression management; that is the atypical-emotion capability (micro-27).
- Do not use when guilt, shame, pride, gratitude, blame, or moral responsibility is the central cause of the emotion; that is the moral-emotion capability (micro-30).
- Do not use when the display is accidental leakage, a spontaneous facial cue, or a physiological reaction rather than deliberately controlled expression.

## Decision Variable
the social display rule governing expression

## Trigger Checklist
- Is there a mismatch, adjustment, or filtering between felt emotion and shown emotion?
- Is the adjustment motivated by a social rule, role, audience, occasion, politeness norm, or expectation?
- Can the character identify who is present and what expression would be acceptable or unacceptable there?
- Does the answer require explaining the outward display rather than only naming the inner feeling?
- Would the display change if the audience, setting, or role changed?

## Workflow
- Confirm the question asks WHY the character shows a particular expression (or why they keep it under control), not merely what they feel inside.
- Identify the character's likely internal emotion from events, goals, and appraisal.
- Identify the audience, relationship, setting, role, or occasion that creates a display expectation.
- State the relevant display rule, such as being polite to a host, staying calm as a leader, not gloating in front of a loser, protecting someone's feelings, or smiling for a photo.
- Name the expression-control tactic the character uses: hide, soften, exaggerate, replace, or neutralize the shown emotion.
- Answer with the social reason for the controlled display. Note that the inner feeling persists - if the character had instead TALKED THEMSELVES OUT of the feeling, that would be the emotion-regulation capability (micro-33), not a display rule.

## Special Case
**Composed display driven by a social role**: When a person's role or audience makes a particular expression expected (a leader stays composed, a host stays gracious, a winner does not gloat), the outward emotion is governed by that display rule even though the true feeling differs.

Scene: Zhao Bo is the president of the skateboard club. While demonstrating in front of the members, he falls and gets hurt. He keeps a confident face instead of showing pain or embarrassment.
Question: Why does Zhao Bo appear confident on the surface?
Answer logic: Zhao Bo privately feels embarrassed, but as club president he is expected to project competence and keep the members' trust. The display rule of his leadership role drives the confident face; that social reason - not the inner embarrassment - is the answer. (If the question had asked what he really feels, the answer would be embarrassed, which is the detect-the-mismatch capability instead.)

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the question can be answered by naming the hidden true emotion without explaining a social rule for the outward expression, exit to the detect-the-mismatch capability - identifying the true inner feeling (micro-28).
- If the character changes the feeling itself through reappraisal, calming, or attention shift rather than controlling the display, exit to the emotion-regulation capability (micro-33).
- If the central puzzle is why the true emotion is unusual for the situation, exit to the atypical-emotion capability (micro-27).
- If the central appraisal is responsibility, wrongdoing, deservedness, or norm violation, exit to the moral-emotion capability (micro-30).
- If the expression is primarily a communicative signal such as a wink, glance, or gesture meant to convey an intention, route to the relevant communication or nonverbal-intention capability instead.
