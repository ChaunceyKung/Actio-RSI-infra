# Trust and Reputation

## Use When
- Use when the question asks how much a person can be counted on in future dealings, judged from what they have done across past interactions.
- Use when a pattern over time of kept or broken promises, honesty, loyalty, or follow-through is what settles the answer.
- Use when the scene gives a track record (for example: 'always', 'every time', 'last three times', 'has done this before') and asks whether to rely on, confide in, or depend on that person again.
- Use when reputation built from repeated behavior is being weighed, not a single claim made right now.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the real question is whether ONE message, rumor, document, or source is believable right now; that is a single-instance credibility judgment about information, not a track record over time about a person - source credibility.
- Do not use when the question is only whether a particular statement is literally true or false - truthfulness of a statement - or whether wording is technically true but misleading - misleading-but-true expression.
- Do not use when the issue is whether one exchange or split is fair or balanced - fairness and reciprocity.
- Do not use when the scene is mainly about blame for harm, apology, repair, status, role, or group membership rather than future reliability.
- Do not use when there is no prior history of the person and the answer must rest on immediate evidence alone.

## Decision Variable
long-term reliability

## Trigger Checklist
- Is the unit of judgment a PERSON's future dependability, not the truth of a single message?
- Does the scene supply a HISTORY (more than one interaction, or words like 'always', 'every time', 'again', 'has before') that shows kept or broken promises, honesty, loyalty, or follow-through?
- Is the question asking whether to rely on, confide in, lend to, or depend on this person going forward?
- Would a repeated pattern outweigh a single isolated act in answering?

## Workflow
- Confirm the unit of judgment is a PERSON's reliability, not the believability of one message. If the prompt asks 'is this claim/message true or accurate', stop and route out to source credibility or truthfulness of a statement.
- Name the person being judged and gather their track record across interactions: promises kept or broken, honesty, loyalty, betrayals, help, consistency, follow-through.
- Separate the repeated pattern from any single isolated event. Count how many times the behavior recurs; one instance is not yet a reputation.
- Weigh the pattern: many reliable acts raise trust; many broken promises or lies lower it. A lone good deed does NOT cancel a long unreliable record, and a lone slip (especially an honest mistake or forgetting) does NOT cancel a long reliable record.
- Answer whether the history supports relying on this person in this relationship going forward, picking the option anchored to the long-term pattern rather than to recency, charm, status, or confidence.

## Special Case
**Single good act does not erase reputation**: A one-time helpful act does not override a long pattern of unreliability unless the question explicitly asks for change in reputation over time.

Scene: A classmate has broken several promises, but once returned a lost wallet.
Question: Should you trust them with an important secret?
Answer logic: The decision variable is long-term reliability. One good act is weaker than repeated broken promises, so the trust judgment should still be low unless there is clear new evidence of sustained change.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to source credibility if the prompt asks whether ONE message, rumor, or source should be believed right now: that judges a single piece of information, not a person's history.
- Exit to truthfulness of a statement or misleading-but-true expression if the question is only whether a particular utterance is true, false, or technically-true-but-deceptive, rather than whether the person is dependable over time.
- Exit to fairness and reciprocity if the issue is whether one exchange or split is balanced, not whether the person can be relied on later.
- Exit if the main issue is harm, blame, apology, status, or group membership, because those are different social variables.
