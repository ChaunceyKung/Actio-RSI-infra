# macro-16 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
how to detect and clarify a live misunderstanding in conversation

## Route Signal
One party visibly takes an utterance differently from what the speaker intended, and the scene shows the misread live: confusion, a reaction to the wrong reading, or repair language like 'What do you mean?', 'I thought you said...', 'No, I meant...'.

## Hard Boundary
- There is an actual mismatch between intended and received meaning to detect, not just a hint to decode while both sides stay aligned.
- Clarification happens in the moment before harm hardens; it is not aftermath repair of a damaged relationship.
- The focus is recovering shared meaning, not apology, persuasion, negotiation, or judging honesty.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not decode only the literal or intended meaning of the utterance while ignoring that the OTHER party took it a different way; the misread itself is the thing to detect.
- Do not jump to an apology or guilt framing when no harm has occurred and a simple clarification would fully resolve the confusion.
- Do not treat every correction or sharp reaction as an insult or a relationship rupture.
- Do not answer the underlying topic the speakers are discussing instead of identifying the move that repairs the mismatch.
- Do not pull the scene into trust, lying, or morality unless the text explicitly raises it.

## Common Failure Modes
- Not noticing a misunderstanding occurred at all: reading the exchange as if both sides share meaning when one party has actually misread the other.
- Decoding the literal or intended meaning correctly but missing that the listener took it differently, so the answer addresses the content rather than the misread.
- Jumping to apology, blame, or relationship repair when only a quick clarification is needed and no harm has landed.
- Fixating on the surface wording and so failing to recover what the speaker actually intended.
- Over-escalating a neutral referent question or a sharp-sounding reply into offense when it is only confused communication.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A passenger offers his seat to a woman, saying 'be careful with your belly,' because he thinks she is pregnant; she snaps back 'I'm just a bit fat,' taking his kind offer as an insult about her weight. | Why: A live mismatch is present: the speaker's intended meaning (kind, assumes pregnancy) and the received meaning (calling her fat) differ, and the task is to detect that misread, so this is in scope.
- Case: A tired visitor tells his host 'I had a long, hot trip' and adds 'I am thirsty,' and you must work out that he is asking for a drink; the host has not misread anything. | Why: Both parties are still aligned and nothing has been misunderstood; the only task is to decode the hint's intended meaning, so it belongs to plain indirect-meaning decoding, not to misunderstanding repair.

## Boundary Stress Test
- The same words appear in two scenes; in one they are merely ambiguous and need a clarifying question, in the other they have already caused real offense that an apology must address; only the still-clarifiable version is in scope.
- A reply sounds sharp or angry, but it is actually the listener clarifying her own need after being misread; do not over-call offense, recognize the live correction.
- An utterance is indirect or hinted, but both speakers understand it the same way; there is no mismatch to repair, so do not route a pure hint-decoding scene here.
- A third party is confused while the original speaker and listener already share meaning; do not treat the bystander's confusion as a misunderstanding between the two aligned parties.

## Generalization Note
This macro covers any in-the-moment exchange where one party has taken an utterance differently from what the speaker intended, and the task is to detect that live mismatch and pick the lightest clarifying move (ask, restate, or correct the false assumption) that restores shared meaning before any harm is done. It does not cover decoding a hint when both sides are still aligned, nor apologizing after meaning was understood and damage has landed.
