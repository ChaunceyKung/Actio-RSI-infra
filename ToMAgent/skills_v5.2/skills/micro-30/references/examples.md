# micro-30 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the responsibility or norm appraisal behind the emotion

## Route Signal
Route here when emotion inference depends on moral responsibility or norm evaluation rather than on simple event-based affect.

## Hard Boundary
- The deciding cue must be a responsibility, blame, credit, fairness, or norm-violation appraisal; pure outcome affect with no such judgment belongs to the basic-emotion-from-an-event capability (micro-26).
- The target is the moral emotion itself (guilt, shame, pride, anger, gratitude), not what someone truly feels beneath a mask, handled by the hidden-emotion capability (micro-28), nor why they show or hide it, handled by the emotional-display-rules capability (micro-29).
- The target is moral appraisal of the actor or recipient, not regret or relief from comparing reality with an alternative outcome, handled by the reality-versus-alternative comparison capability (micro-31).
- The target is feeling produced by judging responsibility, not a feeling caught from another person's emotion, handled by the empathy-and-emotional-contagion capability (micro-32).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not infer guilt whenever someone looks sad.
- Do not infer pride whenever someone succeeds.
- Do not confuse anger at inconvenience with anger at wrongdoing.

## Common Failure Modes
- Replacing moral appraisal with generic negative emotion.
- Mistaking shame for embarrassment when no norm violation is judged.
- Choosing gratitude from politeness instead of from perceived benefit or help.
- Answering with outcome-based emotion when the scene is actually about blame or credit.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A volunteer mistakenly puts a borrowed laptop in the donation box, and later, when asked, realizes the loss her action caused the owner. | Why: In scope. The deciding cue is a self-responsibility appraisal for harm; once she sees her own action caused the loss, the moral emotion is guilt, not a neutral reaction to the event.
- Case: Same person, same lost laptop, but the scene simply states she is upset to discover the laptop is gone, with no question of fault, duty, or who is to blame. | Why: Out of scope. With the responsibility appraisal removed, only a direct event remains, so this routes to the basic-emotion-from-an-event capability (micro-26); the single decisive variable that flipped is whether a blame/responsibility judgment is in play.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same event happened but no one was blamed or credited, would the answer change?
- If the person felt the same way but had no moral reason for it, would this still fit?
- If the question asks only whether the person is upset, this unit is too specific.

## Generalization Note
This unit generalizes across scenes where moral appraisal shapes emotion, including blame, credit, duty, fairness, harm, apology, and appreciation, without depending on any benchmark-specific story.
