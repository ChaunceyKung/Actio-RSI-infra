# micro-24 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the priority order among commitments: which binding promise, ongoing task, or obligation a character must honor first

## Route Signal
Use this unit when a binding prior promise, ongoing task, or schedule constrains the choice. If there is no such binding element and desires merely compete, route to the free-weighing capability instead.

## Hard Boundary
- Commitment Priority requires a BINDING prior promise, ongoing task, or schedule that decides; without one, free weighing of rival goals applies (Goal-Conflict Arbitration, micro-25).
- It ranks obligations, not raw preference strength; the more enjoyable goal does not win just because it is preferred.
- It is not means-end nesting; here the items compete for the same slot rather than one serving the other (Goal Hierarchy, micro-21).
- It is not next-action prediction or intention reading by itself (Action Prediction micro-23; Intention Recognition micro-22).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume the strongest or most fun desire wins; a binding commitment can outrank a stronger want.
- Do not infer the choice from mood or personality when a promise or active task is what sets the order.
- Do not treat two options as equal competitors when one is already promised, scheduled, or underway.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing a binding commitment with simple goal conflict and weighing the two desires freely.
- Answering with the most enjoyable or most urgent-feeling option instead of the most binding one.
- Assuming the paused original plan is abandoned, when the character resumes it after honoring the obligation.
- Using intention or emotion inference when the scene only asks which plan has priority.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: David is loosely planning a BBQ for friends with no fixed time; the elderly then say they are depending on him to repair community facilities today, which he takes on. Question: what does he do after the repair? | Why: A dependency-backed obligation (the elderly counting on him, needed today) outranks a flexible plan with no fixed time, so it decides the order while the BBQ is only postponed and resumed afterward. In-scope: the more binding commitment, not raw preference or recency, governs the sequence.
- Case: Li Hua loves outdoor adventure; Wang Fang prefers the quiet library; they must pick one place for the weekend, with no promise or schedule in play. Question: where do they go? | Why: No binding commitment exists — two preferences simply compete, so the answer is a free weighing of rival goals. Routes to the free-weighing capability (Goal-Conflict Arbitration, micro-25).

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the scene has only a wish and a temptation with no promise, schedule, or active task, this is the wrong unit (free weighing applies).
- If a promise was already canceled or made explicitly flexible, the commitment no longer decides priority.
- If one goal is only a step toward the other (A in order to B), this is means-end structure, not commitment priority.
- If the question asks what someone will likely do next from habit or emotion, route to action prediction unless priority ordering is the explicit ask.

## Generalization Note
This unit generalizes across calendars, promises, deadlines, duties, invitations, and active tasks whenever a binding prior element decides which plan is honored first. The defining test: removing the commitment changes the answer.
