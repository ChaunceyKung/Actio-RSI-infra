# micro-44 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
whether the give-and-take in this already-arranged exchange is balanced and reciprocal, or one-sidedly favors one party

## Route Signal
Route here when the task asks for a verdict on whether an already-arranged exchange is fair - whether one side has received more than it gave back, or is taking advantage.

## Hard Boundary
- Not for designing or bargaining toward a new deal - that is reaching agreement through give-and-take (negotiation).
- Not for assigning blame for harm or deciding whether an apology is owed (responsibility/repair).
- Not for naming the emotion an unfair situation produces (moral emotions).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume every unequal split is unfair - check whether role, prior favor, proportional contribution, or differing capacity justifies the imbalance.
- Do not slide into proposing a fix or compromise; the task is a fairness verdict, not a plan (that would be negotiation).
- Do not answer with how someone feels about the unfairness; that is the emotion, not the fairness judgment (moral emotions).

## Common Failure Modes
- Treating a still-being-negotiated split as a settled one and judging it 'fair/unfair' instead of recognizing the task is to reach a deal (negotiation).
- Answering a fairness question with an emotion word ('Lee should feel guilty') instead of the verdict ('the exchange is unfair').
- Flagging any inequality as exploitation while ignoring a clear justification (an agreed role, a debt being repaid).

## Minimal Pair
- Case: One roommate keeps accepting rides, covered bills, and favors but never reciprocates; ask whether that is fair. | Why: The arrangement is already in place and the task is a verdict on its reciprocity - fairness.
- Case: Two roommates have not yet agreed how to split a new bill and must settle on 50/50 or 60/40. | Why: Nothing is settled; the task is to reach a mutually acceptable split through give-and-take - that is negotiation, not judging an existing exchange.

## Boundary Stress Test
- A scene shows an unfair split AND asks how the wronged person feels: the imbalance is real, but the task is the emotion, so route to moral emotions, not here.
- A scene describes a one-sided favor history AND asks whether to keep relying on that person: the question is future reliability, so route to trust and reputation, not here.

## Generalization Note
This unit delivers norm-based reciprocity verdicts across money, favors, effort, repayment, turn-taking, and burden-sharing. It judges a settled or implied arrangement; it does not design deals (negotiation), rate a person's long-term reliability (trust), assign blame, or name the emotions a fairness judgment triggers (moral emotions).
