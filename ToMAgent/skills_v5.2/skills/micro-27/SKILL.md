# Atypical Emotion

## Use When
- Use when the character's actual emotion is surprising, counter-normative, or not the emotion most people would predict from the event.
- Use when the question asks why someone feels glad, relieved, proud, amused, calm, sad, or angry in a situation where the ordinary expectation points elsewhere.
- Use when the key task is to identify the character-specific appraisal that makes the unusual emotion make sense.
- Use when the emotion is genuinely felt, not merely performed or concealed.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the emotion follows ordinary success, failure, threat, loss, or gain; route to Basic Emotion Appraisal instead.
- Do not use when the person feels one thing internally but shows another externally; route to Hidden Emotion.
- Do not use when the main issue is a social rule for managing expression, such as politeness, professionalism, or saving face; route to Emotional Disguise and Display Rules.
- Do not use when the answer depends mainly on choosing among competing goals or actions rather than explaining an unusual feeling.
- Do not use when the emotion is unusual only to the observer because the observer lacks factual context; first resolve the character's belief or knowledge boundary.

## Decision Variable
the character-specific appraisal that reverses or alters the expected emotion

## Trigger Checklist
- An event has an obvious ordinary emotional prediction.
- The character's actual emotion differs from that ordinary prediction.
- The character is not merely pretending, masking, or following a display rule.
- There is evidence for a special value, desire, belief, past experience, identity concern, or social meaning.
- The question asks why the unusual feeling occurs, not just what the face or behavior displays.

## Workflow
- State the ordinary expected emotion for the event.
- Identify the character's actual or implied emotion.
- Find the appraisal feature that differs for this character: goal relevance, personal value, belief, memory, comparison point, relationship meaning, or identity meaning.
- Explain how that appraisal makes the unusual emotion coherent from the character's perspective.
- Check that the explanation concerns the true feeling itself, not a hidden feeling or managed display.

## Special Case
**An unusual emotion after an event because of the character's own wish, value, or intention**: When the genuine feeling contradicts the default reaction for an event, look for the character-specific reason in the text: a stated wish, a personal value, a prior intention, a comparison point, or a relationship meaning. The feeling is real and felt, not a face put on for others; that is what separates this capability from reading a hidden or disguised emotion.

Scene: Lin Feng dances on stage. As a creative element of his choreography, he deliberately designs a moment where he appears to fall by accident, and he completes the whole dance safely. He then falls during the dance exactly as planned.
Question: What does Lin Feng feel?
Answer logic: Falling in front of an audience normally causes embarrassment. But Lin Feng intended the fall as part of the performance, so relative to his own plan the fall is a success. The genuine feeling is satisfaction. This is atypical because his intention reverses the default appraisal, not because he is hiding embarrassment behind a calm face.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the genuine inner feeling is the ordinary one and the puzzle is that the character shows a different feeling on the outside, exit to the hidden-emotion capability that reads the true feeling behind a managed display (micro-28).
- If the explanation is a social norm about which emotion should be displayed in public, such as politeness or saving face, exit to the display-rule capability for managing emotional expression (micro-29).
- If the emotion turns out to be the ordinary reaction once the success, failure, threat, loss, or gain is identified, exit to the basic-emotion-appraisal capability for default reactions (micro-26).
- If the question asks what action the character chooses among competing motives rather than what they feel, exit to the goal-conflict-arbitration capability that selects an action (micro-25).
- If the feeling only looks unusual because the observer lacks a fact the character knows or falsely believes, resolve the belief or knowledge capability first; the emotion may then be ordinary under that belief.
