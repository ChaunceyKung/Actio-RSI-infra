# micro-47 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
whether group membership changes interpretation or evaluation

## Route Signal
Route here when which group a person belongs to (in-group versus out-group, shared category, stereotype, belonging) is what changes how their act, remark, or character is read. Not here if the decisive cue is one person's rank over another or one person's own track record.

## Hard Boundary
- Group membership (one of us versus one of them), not rank between two individuals (who outranks whom) handled by the power and role unit.
- Category-based judgment of a whole group, not one person's reliability from their own past conduct handled by the trust and reputation unit.
- Belonging or stereotype effect, not whether information may be disclosed handled by the privacy unit, and not mending a relationship after harm handled by the repair unit.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not confuse 'they are the boss' (rank between individuals) with 'they are one of us' (shared group membership, this unit).
- Do not route here just because a group word like team, class, or club appears; route here only when membership in that category is what shifts the judgment.
- Do not infer trust or fault from a single person's history and call it a group effect; a record attached to one person is the trust unit.

## Common Failure Modes
- Routing an interpersonal-rank scene here because both people sit in the same team or office, when the real variable is one person's authority over the other (belongs to power, status, and role).
- Reading a stereotype-driven inference as plain emotion or attitude reading and missing that group membership is what fixed the interpretation.
- Overrouting any scene that merely mentions a group label, when the decisive variable is actually trust, privacy, or repair.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A junior and a senior on the same team get the same blunt note; the junior reads it as fair because the senior is their boss and outranks them. | Why: The driver is rank between two individuals, not membership in opposing groups, so this routes to the power, status, and role unit, not here.
- Case: A new neighbor is assumed to be unfriendly before anyone has met them, simply because they belong to the rival local club. | Why: The judgment comes from category membership and out-group suspicion, with no rank and no personal history, so this is the in-group/out-group case (this unit).

## Boundary Stress Test
- Swap test: if the same act would be judged differently solely because the person belonged to the other group, this unit matches.
- If deleting the group label removes the explanation, group membership was decisive and this unit matches.
- If the scene still reads the same after removing all category cues, the real variable is something else (rank, trust, privacy, or repair), so do not use this unit.

## Generalization Note
This unit covers any shift in interpretation or evaluation driven by which group a person belongs to, including stereotypes, belonging, affiliation, and in-group favoritism or out-group suspicion. The dividing line from the neighbors: group membership categorizes people into us versus them (this unit), whereas rank orders two individuals by authority and reputation tracks one individual's own history.
