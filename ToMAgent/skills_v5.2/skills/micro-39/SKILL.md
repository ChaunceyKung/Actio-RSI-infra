# Speaker Motivation

## Use When
- Use when the question literally asks 'Why does the speaker say this?' and the best answer is an interpersonal reason.
- Use when the answer is a social motive such as sparing the listener's feelings, saving face, maintaining harmony, being polite, making someone laugh, testing, avoiding conflict, or ingratiating.
- Use when the utterance is a white lie, a polite untruth, or a playful exaggeration, and the task is to explain WHY it was said, not whether it is true.
- Use when the literal content could be true or false but the decisive variable is the speaker's relationship-management goal.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the question asks whether the statement is true, false, or a half-truth; that is the truth-status capability (micro-37).
- Do not use when the question asks whether a literally true statement creates a false impression; that is the misleading-expression capability (micro-38).
- Do not use when the goal is to change the listener's belief, attitude, or behavior; that is the persuasion-strategy capability (micro-41).
- Do not use when the task is to fit the message to the listener's age, expertise, or identity; that is the audience-calibration capability (micro-40).
- Do not use when the question asks what the speaker really MEANS or hints at, or what hidden request is being made; that is the implied-meaning capability (micro-36) or the indirect-speech-act capability (micro-35).

## Decision Variable
the speaker's social motive for choosing that utterance form

## Trigger Checklist
- Does the question literally ask why the speaker says it (not what it means and not whether it is true)?
- Is the best answer a motive label such as sparing feelings, saving face, politeness, humor, harmony, testing, or avoidance?
- Could the literal content be untrue (a white lie or joke) yet the speaker still has a kind or social reason?
- Would the social effect change if the wording changed, even with the same facts?

## Workflow
- Confirm the question asks WHY the speaker speaks this way, not what it means or whether it is true.
- State the gap between what the speaker privately thinks and what they say aloud (e.g., dislikes the hat but praises it).
- Name the interpersonal goal that closes that gap: sparing feelings, saving face, keeping harmony, politeness, humor, testing, avoidance, or ingratiation.
- Pick the option that gives the kindest/most relationship-focused motive supported by the story; reject options that just restate the literal content or claim the statement is sincere.
- If the real task is truth-status, false impression, changing the listener, fitting the listener, or decoding a hidden request, exit to that capability.

## Special Case
**True or false content, social motive**: A statement can be a white lie (false) or literally true and still belong here, as long as the question is WHY it was said. The answer is the social motive, never a verdict on the facts.

Scene: In an office meeting Si Xuan is asked if she likes the new boss. She does not really like him, but to keep the office harmonious she answers, 'Yes, I like him very much!'
Question: Why does Si Xuan say this?
Answer logic: The decision variable is the social motive. Si Xuan wants to maintain office harmony and avoid conflict, so she expresses approval she does not feel. Reject options that say her praise is sincere or that her answer does not matter; the point is not whether the boss is likable but why she speaks this way.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to the truth-status capability — is the statement true, false, or a half-truth (micro-37) — if the answer depends on whether the words match the facts.
- Exit to the misleading-expression capability — whether a literally true statement induces a false belief (micro-38) — if the issue is a created false impression rather than a motive.
- Exit to the persuasion-strategy capability — how to change the listener's belief, attitude, or behavior (micro-41) — if the core task is one-way influence on the listener.
- Exit to the audience-calibration capability — fitting wording to the listener's age, expertise, or identity (micro-40) — if the core task is comprehension fit, not motive.
- Exit to the implied-meaning capability — what is communicated but not said (micro-36) — or the indirect-speech-act capability — the hinted request, warning, or refusal (micro-35) — if the question asks what the speaker really means rather than why they said it.
