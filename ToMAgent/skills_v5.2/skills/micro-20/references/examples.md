# micro-20 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the character's representation of another person's preference

## Route Signal
Route here when the scene asks whether one person correctly grasps ANOTHER person's likes or wants — and the right answer suits that other person's taste, not the actor's own.

## Hard Boundary
- The target variable is another person's preference; the focal character's own taste belongs to the character's own preference capability (micro-19).
- The answer must be a LIKING, not the purpose of an action (intention recognition, micro-22) or a predicted next move (action prediction, micro-23).
- This unit is about representing taste, not revising a belief or reading an emotion.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not answer with the ACTOR's own taste when they are choosing for someone else — that collapses other-preference into own-preference.
- Do not pick what the other person will DO next; pick what they LIKE.
- Do not infer preference from emotional tone alone unless the scene links it to what the person likes or wants.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with the actor's own preference instead of the other person's, especially when their tastes differ.
- Treating a wish or liking as an action's purpose (sliding into intention recognition).
- Answering with the most likely next action instead of the represented liking (sliding into action prediction).

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Adventurous Li Hua invites quiet, library-loving Wang Fang for the weekend. Where does Li Hua plan to spend it with her? — the Library. | Why: The answer is driven by Li Hua's model of Wang Fang's quiet taste, set against his own adventurous liking. The decisive variable is one person's representation of another's preference, so this is Other Preference.
- Case: Susan wants the cookies she baked, but is invited to swim; she swims, then after coming home, what does she do? — she eats her cookies. | Why: The query is Susan's OWN desire; no other person's taste decides the answer. This routes to the character's own preference (micro-19).

## Boundary Stress Test
- If two people have opposite tastes and one chooses for the other, verify the answer matches the RECIPIENT's taste, not the chooser's own.
- If the scene gives a gaze, hint, or request and asks 'what does he want to do', that is the purpose behind an act — exit to intention recognition (micro-22).
- If the question is 'what is his next action' after he notices something, that is a predicted behavior — exit to action prediction (micro-23), not a taste judgment.

## Generalization Note
This unit covers stable or momentary likes, wants, and choices, in any domain, as long as the central variable is ONE person's understanding of ANOTHER person's preference.
