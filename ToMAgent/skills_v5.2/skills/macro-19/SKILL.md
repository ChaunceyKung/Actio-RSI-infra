# First Impression, Self-Presentation, and Formal Interaction

## Use When
- Use when the main task is to curate the image one presents of oneself to an audience.
- Use when the scene centers on a first impression, a self-introduction, etiquette, or a formal first meeting.
- Use when the answer depends on choosing what to disclose or withhold, in what order, and in what register to build the intended impression.
- Use when the live decision is how formal, polished, warm, or reserved to seem so the presentation fits the audience and occasion.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the goal is to change the listener's belief or behavior by giving them reasons, offers, or incentives, since that is one-way persuasion about the other party, not curating one's own image.
- Do not use when the live decision is which move to make given a status difference, such as deferring to, challenging, or reporting up or down a hierarchy.
- Do not use when the main issue is apology, compensation, or reconciliation after one has caused harm.
- Do not use when the main issue is bargaining over terms or reaching a mutually acceptable compromise between conflicting sides.
- Do not use when the question is really about what someone hidden feels, knows, or intends rather than how one outwardly presents oneself.

## Decision Variable
how to manage the image one presents to others

## Direct Route Rule
Route immediately when the central decision is how the actor presents their own image to an audience: a first impression, a self-introduction, or the register and content of a formal interaction.

## Trigger Checklist
- A new audience or a formal occasion where the impression one makes matters.
- The actor must choose how formal, polished, warm, or reserved to seem.
- The question asks what to say, disclose, withhold, wear, or do to come across appropriately.
- Who the audience is and what they value shapes the correct response.
- The goal is to curate one's own presented image, not to move the listener's belief or to pick a move set by rank.
- The scene is about the image one shows to others, not the private self behind it.

## Workflow
- Read the audience and the occasion: who they are, what they value, and how formal the setting is, so you know which register and content will land.
- Decide what to emphasize, disclose, or withhold, and in what order, to build the impression the actor intends, for example leading with relevance and credibility rather than dumping everything at once.
- Choose the register and conduct (wording, tone, level of formality and warmth) that match that audience and occasion.
- Select the self-presentation move that builds the intended impression honestly, without boasting, over-disclosing, or misrepresenting who the actor is.
- Answer in terms of the most fitting outward presentation choice, not the actor's hidden feelings, not a later apology, and not an attempt to argue the audience into something.

## Special Case
**Formality is a presentation choice, not a rank problem**: Stay in this scene when formality is chosen to shape how the actor comes across to an audience. Leave it when the formal setting is really about which move to make given a status difference, where the question is whether to defer to, challenge, or report up or down a hierarchy.

Scene: A person is meeting a future employer for the first time and wants to come across as professional and trustworthy.
Question: How should they greet the employer?
Answer logic: Read the audience and occasion (a formal first meeting with someone evaluating them), then choose a greeting that is warm but concise and respectful, leading with relevance rather than over-disclosing. The decision turns on curating one's own first impression, so this scene type fits directly.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the goal is to change the listener's belief or behavior by giving them reasons, offers, or incentives, treat it as one-way persuasion: find the argument or offer that actually moves the other party, rather than asking how the speaker comes across.
- If the live decision is which move to make given a status difference (whether to defer to, push back on, or report up or down a hierarchy), solve it as power-gap communication, weighing rank and standing, not as image curation.
- If the main issue is repairing harm one has caused (apology, making amends, restoring a relationship after an offense), solve it as a repair problem focused on accountability, not self-presentation.
- If the main issue is bargaining over terms (offers, concessions, or a mutually acceptable compromise between conflicting sides), solve it as a negotiation, not as managing one's image.
- If the question is really about what someone hidden feels, knows, or intends, reason about that inner state directly instead of about the outward presentation shown here.
