# macro-15 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
whether the current trust judgment and behavior prediction should be driven by the long-term track record (past reliability, repeated reciprocity, reputation) rather than by any single recent event

## Route Signal
A track record over time — past reliability, repeated reciprocity, or reputation — is what decides how much to trust the person now and what they will likely do.

## Hard Boundary
- A single statement judged on its own, with no relationship history, is a one-off credibility call and does not belong here.
- One isolated lie or one mistake does not by itself create a relationship-history case; there must be an actual track record to weigh.
- A scene whose real task is apology or damage repair after harm belongs to repair reasoning, not to track-record judgment.
- A scene whose real task is a fair allocation of a resource belongs to fairness reasoning, not to track-record judgment.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not let the single most recent event decide the answer while ignoring the longer record.
- Do not treat trust as a pure true-or-false check on the latest statement.
- Do not discard the established baseline the relationship has built up over time.
- Do not treat a one-off statement as if it carried a track record it never had.

## Common Failure Modes
- Over-weighting one recent event (a single late payment, one missed deadline) and forgetting a long history of reliability.
- Treating a single statement or a brand-new acquaintance as if there were an established track record to trust.
- Ignoring reputation and third-party signals that should inform the trust judgment.
- Collapsing a fair-allocation or after-harm-repair scene into trust-from-history, or vice versa, instead of using the matching tool.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A manager decides whether to hand a sensitive account to an assistant who has been careful and reliable for years, after the assistant missed one report following a family emergency. | Why: There is a real multi-year track record, and the call is whether one excusable lapse overrides it — exactly trust integrated over a history.
- Case: A manager has just met a contractor with no prior dealings and must decide whether to believe a single claim on their resume, with no track record to draw on. | Why: Near miss: no history exists, so this is a one-off credibility judgment about a single statement, decided from its content and the speaker's motive — not from an accumulated record.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the scene offers only one statement from someone with no shared history, this is a single-statement credibility call, not a track-record judgment.
- If the scene is about whether to apologize, compensate, or reconcile after a harm, it is a repair problem, not a judgment from the long-term record.
- If the scene is about splitting something fairly this one time, it is a fairness decision, not a reading of the relationship's trust history.
- If the scene asks what a remark means, do not solve it here unless the deciding factor is genuinely the trust built up over time.

## Generalization Note
This macro generalizes to any scene where an accumulated interaction history — repeated reliability, honored reciprocity, or reputation — changes how much to trust someone now and what to predict they will do, even when the wording or surface format varies.
