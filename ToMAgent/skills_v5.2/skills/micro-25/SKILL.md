# Goal-Conflict Arbitration

## Use When
- Use when two or more goals are active at once and the answer is which one the character weights more strongly (and often why).
- Use when a character acts against a surface desire and you must name the competing goal that outweighed it (e.g., wants to play but stays in to avoid being mocked).
- Use when the goals freely compete with no binding prior promise, ongoing task, or schedule deciding the order.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when a binding prior promise, ongoing task, or schedule decides which plan is honored first; that is Commitment Priority (micro-24).
- Do not use when one goal is merely a step done in order to reach another (means-end nesting); that is Goal Hierarchy (micro-21).
- Do not use when the question asks what the character will physically do next from belief and goal; that is Action Prediction (micro-23).
- Do not use when the issue is how the character feels about a gain, loss, threat, or blame; that is an emotion-appraisal capability (micro-26 onward).
- Do not use when the issue is reading intention, belief, persuasion, or social meaning rather than weighing goals.

## Decision Variable
the weighting rule for competing goals: which active goal the character gives greater weight, and on what basis

## Trigger Checklist
- Two or more goals are active at the same time and pull toward different choices.
- The question asks which goal wins, outweighs, or dominates — or why the character chose against a stated desire.
- No binding prior promise, ongoing task, or schedule pre-settles the choice (removing one would not change the answer).
- The answer depends on a tradeoff: salience, urgency, reward, cost, fear, social image, or value.

## Workflow
- Identify every active goal in the scene, including a hidden competing goal behind a surprising choice (e.g., avoiding embarrassment, not worrying a parent).
- Confirm no binding prior promise, ongoing task, or schedule already decides the order; if one does, this is the wrong unit.
- Infer the weighting rule: which goal carries more weight and on what basis (reward, cost, fear, social image, urgency, value).
- Answer with the goal the rule makes dominant, or the reason that competing goal outweighed the surface desire.

## Special Case
**Hidden competing goal behind a contradictory action**: When a character clearly wants X but says or does not-X, look for a second active goal (avoiding embarrassment, protecting someone's feelings, dodging a consequence) that outweighs X. The answer is the weighting rule, not the surface desire.

Scene: Li Lei loves basketball but is not very good and his sister always mocks his skills. When his friend invites him to play, and his sister asks if he is going, Li Lei answers, "No."
Question: Li Lei wants to play basketball, so why does he say no?
Answer logic: Two goals are active: play basketball (the surface desire) and avoid his sister's mockery. With no promise or schedule in play, the second goal outweighs the first, so he says no because he does not want his sister to mock him anymore (the weighting rule), not because he stopped wanting to play.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to the binding-commitment capability if a prior promise, ongoing task, or schedule decides which plan is honored first (Commitment Priority, micro-24).
- Exit to the means-end-structure capability if one goal is merely a step done in order to reach another rather than a rival competing for the same slot (Goal Hierarchy, micro-21).
- Exit to the next-action-prediction capability if the question asks what the character will physically do next rather than how goals are weighted (Action Prediction, micro-23).
- Exit to an emotion-appraisal capability if the scene is about how the character feels about gain, loss, threat, blame, or success (micro-26 onward).
- Exit to the intention-reading or belief capability if the scene is about inferring a purpose or belief without active goal competition (Intention Recognition micro-22, or a belief unit).

## Quick Route Signal
Use this unit when the answer depends on which of several competing goals the character weights higher, with no binding commitment pre-settling it. If a prior promise or schedule decides, route elsewhere.
