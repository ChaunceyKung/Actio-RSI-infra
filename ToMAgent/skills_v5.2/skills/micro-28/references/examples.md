# micro-28 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
internal emotion versus external expression

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when a scene contrasts a character's private emotional state with a different outward expression and the question asks what they truly feel.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit DETECTS the mismatch and names the hidden feeling; it does not explain the social rule that drives the masking (that is the display-rule capability, micro-29).
- This unit reports a concealed feeling; it does not cover changing the feeling itself through reappraisal or calming (that is the emotion-regulation capability, micro-33).
- This unit does not decide whether the character should have hidden the emotion.
- This unit does not classify lies or strategic misdirection unless the lie is only evidence of an emotional display.
- This unit does not infer moral emotions unless the central variable is still the hidden-versus-shown emotion contrast (responsibility-driven guilt belongs to the moral-emotion capability, micro-30).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not trust the visible smile, calm tone, or verbal 'I'm fine' as the true emotion when private evidence points the other way.
- Do not assume every hidden emotion is sadness; infer the private feeling from the character's goal, threat, loss, or concern.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with the displayed emotion (the smile, the calm, the 'I'm fine') instead of the private emotion.
- Drifting into WHY the character masks - explaining politeness or role rules - when the question only asks what the character really feels.
- Treating an unusual but openly expressed emotion as a hidden emotion.
- Ignoring later private behavior or stated motive that reveals the internal state.
- Over-reading concealment when the scene provides no evidence of a mismatch.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Mingming dislikes the long visit to his grandfather but keeps a happy face; asked 'What are Mingming's real feelings?' the answer is annoyed. | Why: The question asks for the inner feeling behind a contrary display, so detecting the hidden emotion is exactly this unit.
- Case: Same Mingming scene, but asked 'Why does Mingming seem happy on the surface?' the answer is 'so his grandfather is not hurt.' | Why: This routes to the social-display-rule capability (micro-29): the target is the social reason for managing the display, not the hidden feeling itself.

## Boundary Stress Test
- Mingming keeps a happy face during a boring visit: answer 'annoyed' here when the question asks what he feels; route to the display-rule capability (micro-29) when it asks why he looks happy.
- A child laughs after losing a toy because she truly thinks the loss is funny: use the atypical-emotion capability, not this unit, since the laughter is the real feeling.
- Chen Fei wins a card game but stays calm so his friend keeps playing: answer the hidden feeling (happy) here; route to the display-rule capability (micro-29) when the question asks why he stays calm.
- After being slighted, a boy tells himself he is a 'secret weapon' and turns his attention to the next game: route to the emotion-regulation capability (micro-33), because he changes the feeling rather than masking it.
- A person hides guilt after breaking a promise: use the moral-emotion capability (micro-30) if responsibility and guilt are the core inference; use this unit only if the task is purely contrasting a felt emotion with a neutral display.

## Generalization Note
Across benchmark and real-world cases, separate the evidence for private appraisal from evidence for public display, then answer the diagnostic question by comparing the two.
