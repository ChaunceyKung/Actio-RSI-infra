# micro-36 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the implicit information derived from context

## Route Signal
Use this unit when the diagnostic target is the extra information communicated implicitly, recovered by a cooperative listener from context, especially scalar or quantity implicatures where the words alone underdetermine the answer.

## Hard Boundary
- The output is an inferred extra proposition or quantity (IMPLY), not the speech act being performed (the Indirect Speech Act capability, micro-35).
- The output is content, not a true-or-false verdict on the sentence (the Truth, Lie, and Half-Truth capability, micro-37).
- If the inference the listener is meant to draw is a FALSE belief the speaker is counting on, the target is misleading expression instead (Misleading and Selective Expression, micro-38).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not just restate the literal sentence in other words; the answer must add the implicit content.
- Do not confuse 'what was meant' (implied fact) with 'what action was done' (speech act).
- Do not treat every context-based inference as implicature; use it only when the target is the extra communicated content.
- For quantity items, do not report only the count in the sample; project the scalar cue onto the whole set.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing an implied fact with an indirect request or warning (mistaking IMPLY for DO).
- Judging truthfulness instead of recovering the inferred meaning.
- Giving a paraphrase of the literal sentence rather than the implied message.
- On scalar items, answering the literal sample count instead of the estimated total.
- Over-inferring beyond what a cooperative listener would reasonably derive.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: In a zoo of 9 monkeys, almost one third are female; an observer counts 8 and finds 2 female, and the question asks how many females are in the zoo overall. | Why: The cooperative inference projects 'almost one third of 9' onto the whole zoo to recover the implied quantity (about 2); the target is that implied amount, so it is in scope for this capability.
- Case: Reaching the candy aisle, a child says 'Look, those candies look delicious', and the question asks what the child really wants. | Why: Here the target is the act the utterance performs (an indirect request to buy candy), not an inferred fact, so it routes to the social-action-of-an-utterance capability (Indirect Speech Act, micro-35), not this unit.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same words are used but the speaker is getting the listener to act, switch to the social-action capability (Indirect Speech Act, micro-35).
- If the statement could be literally true while pushing the listener to a false conclusion, switch to the misleading-true-statement capability (Misleading and Selective Expression, micro-38).
- If the main question is whether the speaker lied, switch to the truth-status capability (Truth, Lie, and Half-Truth, micro-37).
- If you find yourself only paraphrasing the words, you have not yet recovered the implicature; add the inferred content.

## Generalization Note
This is the IMPLY member of the pragmatics triad: SAY (Literal Meaning, micro-34), DO (Indirect Speech Act, micro-35), IMPLY (this unit). It covers conversational enrichment, the extra proposition or quantity a cooperative listener recovers from context, without rewording the literal sentence or naming the social act.
