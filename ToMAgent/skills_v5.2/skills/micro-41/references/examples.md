# micro-41 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the one-way influence strategy directed at the listener

## Route Signal
Use this capability when the question asks which persuasive tactic, appeal, framing, or influence approach is best for changing a listener's mind, feeling, or behavior.

## Hard Boundary
- Not the speaker's motive for speaking (that is the speaker-motivation capability, micro-39).
- Not fitting wording to the listener's level alone (that is the audience-calibration capability, micro-40).
- Not negotiation or mutual concession (that is the negotiation capability, micro-51).
- Not offense or blame judgment (that is the faux-pas-and-offense capability, micro-42, or the harm-and-responsibility capability, micro-43).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not answer with the topic of the message; answer with the tactic used to change the listener.
- Do not confuse a polite tone with persuasion; politeness can be audience fit or a social motive.
- Do not treat a request as persuasion when the main job is to reach a shared agreement through trades.
- Do not pick a merely informative or self-focused statement over the appeal that targets this listener's specific objection or values.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing the speaker's reason for speaking with the tactic aimed at the listener.
- Choosing audience-calibrated wording when the real question is which argument changes the listener.
- Calling a negotiation scene persuasion because one side wants the other to agree, despite reciprocal offers.
- Selecting a generic appeal instead of the one matched to the listener's stated worry or value.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Xiao Hong wants the amusement park; her dad objects to the noise; the winning move is to stress how long it has been and how special the wish is to her. | Why: The decisive variable is which one-way appeal best changes the dad's decision; the listener makes no counter-offer.
- Case: Two roommates negotiate chores: one offers to cook twice a week if the other vacuums, and the other counters by also asking for weekend laundry help. | Why: Near miss: both sides change terms via reciprocal offers to reach a shared plan, so the central variable is bilateral concession. Route to the negotiation capability (micro-51).

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the listener is not being asked to change anything, do not use this capability.
- If both sides trade offers and must agree on terms, route to the negotiation capability (micro-51).
- If the question is only about making the message understandable for the listener, route to the audience-calibration capability (micro-40).
- If the question is why the speaker said it, route to the speaker-motivation capability (micro-39).

## Generalization Note
This capability covers any one-way influence attempt — reasoning, evidence, emotional appeal, appeal to what the listener values, authority, framing, repetition, social proof, reassurance, or incentive — as long as the central variable is the strategy used to change the listener and no reciprocal concession is required.
