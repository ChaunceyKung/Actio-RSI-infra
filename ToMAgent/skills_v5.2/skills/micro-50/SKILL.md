# Self-Presentation and Impression Management

## Use When
- Use when the central question is what impression the actor wants OTHERS to form of THEM (competent, likable, modest, credible).
- Use when the key lever is how the actor shapes self-information: what to reveal first, what to emphasize or omit, how to describe themselves.
- Use when the actor manages their own overall image or identity for an audience (bio, introduction, interview, first impression, profile).
- Use when the same true facts told in a different order or framing would change how the actor is seen.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the actor is hiding or softening one FEELING to be polite, fit a role, or protect another person's feelings (the gift is ugly but they say 'I love it'); that is emotion display rules, even if an image-flavored answer is offered as a distractor.
- Do not use when the question is only WHY a single utterance is phrased a certain way (politeness, saving face, probing) rather than what self-image is being built; that is speaking motive.
- Do not use when the actor is one-way persuading someone about an EXTERNAL claim or request (go to the park, lend the laptop); that is persuasion, not self-presentation.
- Do not use when the main issue is whether information should stay private at all; use privacy and boundaries instead.
- Do not use when the main issue is apology or repairing harm after an offense; use relationship repair instead.

## Decision Variable
what impression of THE SPEAKER the speaker wants others to form, and how they shape self-information to get it

## Trigger Checklist
- Is the target of management the SPEAKER'S OWN image, not another person's feelings and not an external proposal?
- Is the lever the shaping of self-information: order of disclosure, emphasis, omission, or self-description?
- Could the same true facts, reordered or reframed, change how the speaker is seen?
- Is the goal a desired identity (competent, modest, likable, credible), not the concealment of a single emotion?
- Is the scope the broad self-picture, not one protected secret, one apology, or one persuasion target?

## Workflow
- Identify WHO is being managed: the speaker's own image (this unit), another person's feelings (emotion display rules), or someone's decision about an external claim (persuasion).
- If it is the speaker's own image, confirm the lever is shaping self-information: order, emphasis, omission, or self-description.
- Name the desired impression the speaker is building (competent, modest, likable, credible).
- Reject distractors that frame the motive as sparing another's feelings or as winning a request, unless the scene's pivot really is the speaker's self-image.
- Watch the trap: a white-lie to protect SOMEONE ELSE is NOT self-presentation even when an answer option says 'to build a good image'; pick image only when the speaker's own standing is the actual driver.
- Choose the option whose motive is the impression the speaker wants others to form of THEM.

## Special Case
**Truthful facts, reordered for image vs. an image-flavored distractor on a kindness lie**: Two traps sit on opposite sides. (1) Truthful statements ARE self-presentation when the pivot is which facts to lead with, emphasize, or omit to signal an identity. (2) But when someone tells a kind lie to spare ANOTHER person's feelings, an answer option that says 'to build her own good image' is a DISTRACTOR, not the gold; the driver is the other person's feelings.

Scene: Aunt Wang dislikes Uncle Zhang's dish but tells him it is the most delicious she has ever eaten; one answer says she does it to build her own good image in the community.
Question: Is this self-presentation, and why?
Answer logic: No. The pivot is sparing Uncle Zhang's embarrassment, so it is emotion display rules / sparing feelings. The 'build her own image' option is a self-presentation distractor that is wrong, because her OWN standing is not what is actually driving the lie.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the pivot is hiding or softening ONE feeling to be polite, fit a role, or spare another's feelings, exit to the skill for emotion display rules / sparing feelings; this is the most common trap.
- If the pivot is only WHY a single utterance is phrased that way (politeness, saving face, probing), exit to the skill for speaking motive at the utterance level.
- If the pivot is one-way persuasion of someone about an external claim or request, exit to the skill for persuasion.
- If the core question becomes whether information should be shared at all, exit to the skill for privacy and boundaries.
- If the core question becomes how to respond after an offense or damage, exit to the skill for repair and apology.
- If the scene is a full first-impression or formal-interaction situation rather than a single self-presentation diagnostic, treat self-presentation as one component and apply the broader social-interaction capability.
