# micro-25 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the weighting rule for competing goals: which active goal the character gives greater weight, and on what basis

## Route Signal
Use this unit when the answer depends on which of several competing goals the character weights higher, with no binding commitment pre-settling it. If a prior promise or schedule decides, route elsewhere.

## Hard Boundary
- Goal-Conflict Arbitration is FREE weighing of competing goals; if a binding promise, ongoing task, or schedule pre-resolves the choice, route to the binding-commitment capability (Commitment Priority, micro-24).
- It treats goals as parallel rivals for the same slot, not as nested means-and-ends; nesting is Goal Hierarchy (micro-21).
- It outputs which goal is weighted higher (and why), not the next observable behavior, which is Action Prediction (micro-23).
- It is goal weighting at the desire level, not feeling about the outcome, which is an emotion-appraisal capability (micro-26 onward).

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not take the surface desire at face value; find the competing goal that outweighed it.
- Do not assume a promise or schedule unless the scene explicitly states one; inventing a commitment turns this into the wrong unit.
- Do not convert a motivation question into an emotion question or a next-action question.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing free goal weighting with next-step prediction.
- Importing a binding commitment the scene never states, then ranking by obligation instead of weight.
- Stopping at the stated desire and missing the hidden competing goal that actually drove the choice.
- Answering with an emotion or a personality label instead of the active weighting rule.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Li Lei loves basketball but his sister mocks his skill; when his friend invites him and his sister asks, he says no. Question: why does he say no? | Why: Two free goals compete (play vs. avoid mockery) with no promise or schedule; the answer is the weighting rule — avoiding mockery outweighs playing. In-scope free goal weighting.
- Case: Leo wants to rest but already promised his sister he would drive her to the airport tonight, and she has no backup ride. Question: what should decide the priority? | Why: A binding prior promise is present, so the order is set by the commitment rather than by free weighing. Routes to the binding-commitment capability (Commitment Priority, micro-24).

## Boundary Stress Test
- If removing a stated promise, task, or schedule would change the answer, this is the binding-commitment capability, not free weighing.
- If one goal is only a step toward the other (A in order to B), this is means-end structure, not goal conflict.
- If the question can be answered by naming the next physical action alone, route to action prediction.
- If the decisive clue is a feeling word rather than a goal tradeoff, route to an emotion-appraisal capability.

## Generalization Note
This unit covers free goal tradeoffs across everyday, social, moral, and self-protective motives, including hidden competing goals behind contradictory actions, as long as no binding prior commitment resolves the choice.
